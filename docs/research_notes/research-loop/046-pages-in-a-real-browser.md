## In short

- **Ofcom's own gaming guidance confirms 038's reading.**
  - The guidance is dated 14 October 2025.
  - User-to-user content in a game includes "player profiles, avatars, objects, and the environments themselves", and text and voice
    chat.
  - Content the studio publishes is out of scope, and so is offline play.
  - The risks it names first for games are terrorism, grooming, hate and harassment. Players aged 13 to 17 fear trolling most (47%).
- **The risk assessment must cover two new priority offences.** These are serious self-harm and cyberflashing, finalised in June 2026 and
  due in autumn 2026, subject to Parliament. Ofcom's Additional Safety Measures statement, also due in autumn 2026, applies to "All OS
  services". Since 7 April 2026, every regulated service must report child sexual abuse material it detects to the National Crime Agency.
- **A UK ban on social media for under-16s comes next year.**
  - The government announced it in June 2026. Its first regulations are due by the end of 2026. Ofcom will consult on guidance once
    they're laid, and named four age checks it judges "highly effective" at 16.
  - The UK says it will use "the same model for a social media ban as Australia". Australia's rules, in force since 10 December 2025,
    exclude online gaming.
  - My reading: a game with clans is likely outside the UK ban too. This is unsettled until the UK's regulations appear.

## Ofcom's gaming guidance

[Gaming: know the online safety risks, the rules, and how to comply](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/the-online-safety-act-and-gaming-know-the-risks-know-the-rules-know-how-to-comply), 14 October 2025.

- **In scope:** a service with UK links where users can generate, share or upload content that others may meet, "This includes services
  that enable user interactions". In games this means profiles, avatars, objects, environments, text and voice chat, matchmaking that
  connects strangers, and livestreams.
- **Out of scope:** content the provider publishes, offline play, and the enforcement of PEGI ratings.
- **The risks it cites:**
  - Of 13 to 17-year-olds, 47% are highly concerned about trolling, 45% about one-off abuse or threats and 37% about griefing.
  - The NSPCC names voice and text chat in multiplayer games among grooming's routes.
  - Games are the third likeliest place for "nasty or hurtful" behaviour among 8 to 17-year-olds, at 12% (social media 16%, messaging
    15%).
- **Illegal harms with a raised risk in games:** terrorism, child sexual exploitation and abuse (grooming above all), hate, and
  harassment. A provider assesses 17 kinds of illegal content and 12 kinds of content harmful to children.
- **For Fantasy Idle.** Clan names, tags, descriptions and "looking for" lines are text that users write and others meet, so 038's
  reading stands. The game has no chat, profiles, avatars or matchmaking. That keeps the risk low, and it is why chat, DMs and uploads
  stay out (PROPOSALS, deliberate choices) and links stay out of clan words (P-125).

## The UK's under-16 social media ban

- **Ofcom, 29 October 2026** ([news](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/how-age-checks-at-age-16-can-work-to-underpin-uks-social-media-ban)):
  - The government announced a ban for under-16s in June and will "set out how the ban will work in practice" by the end of 2026.
  - It comes into force next year.
  - Ofcom found four checks "highly effective" at 16: photo ID matching, digital ID, banking-based checks, and facial age estimation with a
    challenge age. Ofcom will consult on guidance once the regulations are laid.
  - The government plans "the same model for a social media ban as Australia", with stronger age checks.
- **Australia's model** ([eSafety](https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions)), in force
  since 10 December 2025:
  - It covers platforms whose sole or a significant purpose is online social interaction, where users link to each other and post
    material.
  - "Online gaming and standalone messaging apps are among a number of types of services that have been excluded under the Rules."
  - Ten platforms named, from Facebook to Reddit.
  - Penalties up to AUD 109.2 million.
- **For Fantasy Idle.** If the UK copies Australia's exclusion, the ban doesn't reach the game. Its clans exist to play together, not as
  a social network. That's my reading, and the regulations aren't out yet. It joins 038's and 045's watch list. Turkey's under-15 rule
  for social network providers (045) is the nearest live case, and P-048's lawyer has it.
