# 038: Online-safety and platform law for a small game (read 11 November 2026)

Read from legislation.gov.uk (the Online Safety Act as amended to November 2026), GOV.UK (Ofcom's codes as laid before Parliament, and the memorandum on their 2026 amendments), EUR-Lex (the Digital Services Act, the EU KIDS Act proposal), the Commission's site (today's Digital Fairness Act proposal, the minors guidelines page), and notes by Bristows, Slaughter and May, Lewis Silkin, Bird & Bird, Osborne Clarke, IFTAS, Inforrm, Euronews, Agence Europe, RTÉ, Freevacy and Help Net Security.
Ofcom's own site answered every automated read with a browser check, as in 030, so its guide pages, regulation checker and list of fines were not read; Ofcom's codes are read as laid on GOV.UK instead. White & Case, Covington, Quartz, Massively OP and Digital Watch refused too. No page carried instructions aimed at a reader like me. Held against the code at the worktree's tip.
**This is not legal advice.** It maps the ground for the owner and for a lawyer; the questions at the end are for one.

## In short

- **The UK Act very likely sees Fantasy Idle as a user-to-user service.** Clan names, tags, descriptions and "looking for" lines are written by one player and read by others ([s.3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/3)); the one exemption shaped like a game covers comments on the provider's own content ([Sch. 1 para 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/schedule/1/paragraph/4)). Whether it is *regulated* turns on UK links ([s.4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/4)), which nobody can check: the server keeps no countries.
- **If in scope, a small, low-risk service owes little, but in writing:** a risk assessment and a children's access assessment within three months of its first day in scope (then a children's risk assessment if children are likely users), records, and 14 code measures (a named person, review and swift removal, complaints, terms). No age checks, no scanning, no fee. IFTAS puts the paperwork at 4–8 hours.
- **Ofcom has fined small services, though none I found was small and low-risk:** an image host with 855 UK users was fined £20,000 for unanswered information requests; 4chan's £520,000 included £50,000 for its risk assessment and £20,000 for its terms.
- **The DSA reaches the game partly, if at all.** Whether a free game with nothing sold is an "information society service" is disputed, and a provider outside the EU needs a substantial connection to it. If it applies, a micro enterprise owes contact points, terms, notice and action, statements of reasons and, from outside the EU, a legal representative; reports and every online-platform duty, minors' protection included, are waived.
- **Two EU proposals now name games.** The KIDS Act (17 September) would make online games of any size minor-safe by default, with no "penalties or loss of benefits for failing to engage regularly"; the Digital Fairness Act (today) would bar pay-to-win and paid loot boxes for minors, and let adults switch such clocks off. Both are years from applying.
- **A minimum plan is two to three days of work:** a rules page, a short risk note, P-110's address and report link, P-113's edits with an owner's way to clear a clan's words, and no chat, DMs or uploads.

## 1. The UK's Online Safety Act

**Is it a user-to-user service?**
- The test: content a user writes that may be encountered by other users; "it does not matter what proportion of content" is theirs ([s.3(1)–(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/3)).
- Fantasy Idle's player text (checked): usernames of 3–24 letters, digits, _ or - (`api/index.js:19`); clan names of 3–32 letters, digits, spaces, ' _ - and tags of 2–5 (`:489-490`); a description of 200 and a "looking for" line of 100 characters, cleaned only of control characters and angle brackets (`:500`, `:832`, `api/store.js:31-32`). Any signed-in player can read them in the clan list (`api/index.js:798-803`, `src/ui/render.js:2405`); names and tags also show in the ranking (`:2691`). No chat, DMs, images or player-written profiles; the hero's name stays private (030). Anyone with an email gets an account.
- The exemptions don't fit: email, SMS, one-to-one voice, internal business tools, public bodies, schools, and services where users can only comment on, react to or rate the provider's content ([Sch. 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/schedule/1)). A clan description comments on nothing. Usernames alone are "identifying content" ([s.236](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/236)); here they are not alone.
- Ofcom's gaming guidance (October 2025) puts profiles, avatars, objects, environments, text and voice chat, matchmaking and livestreams in scope, and studio content, offline play and PEGI ratings out ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/10/24/ofcom-issues-guidance-about-online-safety-act-in-video-gaming-context-102lr5v), [Bird & Bird](https://mediawrites.twobirds.com/post/102lrmy/patch-notes-ofcom-releases-new-guidance-on-the-impact-of-osa-on-gaming-industry)). Clans aren't named; by the Act's words, clan text is in (my reading).
- The provider is whoever controls who can use the user-to-user part ([s.226](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/226)): the owner. The Discord server is Discord's service, moderated by the owner under Discord's rules (my reading of the same section).

**Is it regulated?** Only with UK links: a "significant number" of UK users, the UK as a target market, or a material risk of significant harm to people there ([s.4(5)–(6)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/4)). No number is set ([IFTAS](https://about.iftas.org/2025/07/30/navigating-the-uk-online-safety-act/)), and being based abroad doesn't help ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/10/24/ofcom-issues-guidance-about-online-safety-act-in-video-gaming-context-102lr5v)). Fantasy Idle has 45 accounts (027), English only, no UK-specific price, domain or marketing that I found, and no country data (023). **Unclear.** With 200-character clan text, s.4(6) looks unlikely (my judgement). The cheap course is to act as if in scope. Ofcom's [regulation checker](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/os-toolkit/regulation-checker/regulation-checker) could not be opened.

**If in scope, what and when.**
- A new service completes its first illegal-content risk assessment and children's access assessment within three months of its first day in scope ([Sch. 3 para 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/schedule/3)). Clans have run on the database since at least 11 October 2026 (ROADMAP); if in scope from then, both fall due about mid-January 2027. Unclear: the first day depends on UK links.
- The duties: a suitable and sufficient risk assessment, kept up to date and redone before a significant change ([s.9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/9)); written records of it and of the measures used ([s.23](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/23)); easy reporting of illegal content ([s.20](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/20)), including intimate-image reports since 29 June 2026 ([s.20A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/20A)); complaints easy to use "including by children" ([s.21](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/21)); reporting detected child sexual abuse content to the National Crime Agency, in force since 7 April 2026 ([s.66](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/66)).
- Children: the access assessment asks whether children can use the service and whether many do or would. Only age verification or estimation can rule children out ([s.35](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/35)); a record is kept ([s.36(7)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/36)); skipping it counts as "likely to be accessed" ([s.37(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/37)). A children's risk assessment then follows within three months (Sch. 3 para 5) and must cover features that affect how much children use the service ([s.11(6)(f)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/11)): the game's clocks belong in it.

**What the codes ask of a small, low-risk service.** Following a code counts as complying ([s.49](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50/section/49)). A service is large above 7 million monthly UK users, and multi-risk at medium or high risk of two or more kinds of illegal harm ([illegal content code as laid](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6762c7dfff2c870561bde79f/draft-illegal-content-codes-of-practice-for-user-to-user-services.pdf), §5.6 and definitions). Fourteen measures apply to a service that is neither (its index, §3):
- ICU A2, a named person accountable for the illegal-content and complaints duties; C1–C2, review of suspected illegal content and swift takedown;
- D1, D2, D7, D9–D13, a findable complaints route that acts, decides appeals and may set aside manifestly unfounded complaints;
- G1 and G3, terms saying how users are protected, what proactive technology is used and how complaints are handled, written clearly; H1, removing accounts of proscribed organisations.
- Services likely to be accessed by children add the same shape from the [children's code as laid](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/680a04f7532adcaaab3a2718/FINAL_-_Protection_of_Children_Code_of_Practice_for_user-to-user_services__2025_Parli_AC.pdf): PCU A2, C1–C2, D1–D2, D7 and the appeal measures, G1 and G3. Bristows counts the same 14 baseline measures and finds no small-service exemption ([note](https://inquisitiveminds.bristows.com/post/102k11s/my-organisation-runs-a-small-online-forum-is-it-in-scope-of-the-osa)).
- Age checks: highly effective age assurance is recommended only where pornography, self-harm, suicide, eating-disorder or other priority content is the service's purpose, is allowed, can't be taken down, or is recommended by a feed (PCU B2–B7, same code). A game whose terms ban it, and whose owner can remove any clan text, needs none.
- 2026 additions: crisis-response protocols reach small services only when high risk; they apply to large medium-risk and any-size high-risk services ([GOV.UK memorandum, 18 June 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/online-safety-act-draft-amendments-to-the-protection-of-children-code-of-practice-for-user-to-user-services/explanatory-memorandum-draft-amendments-no-2-to-the-illegal-content-codes-of-practice-for-user-to-user-services-and-draft-amendments-to-the-protec)). Intimate-image hash matching was laid on 1 June (whom it covers: not checked; the game hosts no images). The rest of Ofcom's 2025 package was due in autumn 2026: not checked.
- No size floor for the duties ([Bristows](https://inquisitiveminds.bristows.com/post/102k11s/my-organisation-runs-a-small-online-forum-is-it-in-scope-of-the-osa)). Fees start at £250 million worldwide revenue, and under £10 million of UK revenue is exempt ([GOV.UK, 20 November 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/implementation-of-the-online-safety-act-fees-threshold-and-exemptions-to-fee-paying/implementation-of-the-online-safety-act-fees-threshold-and-exemptions-to-fee-paying-letter-from-dsit-secretary-of-state-to-ofcom)).

**What small services do, and what Ofcom has done.**
- Comply on paper: IFTAS's guide for volunteer-run servers asks for a documented risk assessment, a named role, a monitored address, reports by form or email, and terms that ban illegal content; no proactive scanning for a small, low-risk service; 4–8 hours of effort. It links sample assessments and a server's published ones ([IFTAS](https://about.iftas.org/2025/07/30/navigating-the-uk-online-safety-act/)).
- Or leave: a cycling forum and a forum host announced closures (030), and Gab and Civitai blocked UK users ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_Safety_Act_2023)).
- Fines so far, all on high-risk services or ones that ignored Ofcom: Im.ge, an image host with 855 UK users, £20,000 in December 2025 for two unanswered information requests, its risk assessment still under investigation ([Slaughter and May](https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102miup/how-does-ofcom-set-penalties-under-the-osa-insights-from-its-recent-decisions), [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/12/18/december-online-safety-roundup-ofcom-issues-fines-and-guidance-ico-targets-chil-102ly62)); 4chan, £520,000 on 19 March 2026: £450,000 for age checks, £50,000 for the risk assessment, £20,000 for terms, unpaid ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/19/uk-ofcom-4chan-age-checks-fine/)); a suicide forum, £950,000 (May 2026, [Freevacy](https://www.freevacy.com/news/ofcom/ofcom-fines-unnamed-suicide-forum-ps950000-investigates-two-adult-websites/7373)); pornography sites up to £1.35 million ([Inforrm](https://inforrm.org/2026/03/11/ofcom-steps-up-online-safety-act-enforcement-with-two-further-age-assurance-fines-for-pornographic-platforms-alexandros-antoniou/)). The cap is £18 million or 10% of worldwide revenue (Slaughter and May).
- The lesson I draw: Ofcom's first step in both small cases was a formal request for information. A service that keeps its records (s.23) and answers stands apart from one that doesn't.

## 2. The EU's Digital Services Act

- **Does it apply at all? Two open doors.** It covers intermediary services, which are information society services ([DSA art. 3(a), (g)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_3)): "normally provided for remuneration" ([Directive 2015/1535 art. 1(1)(b)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32015L1535#art_1)), including unpaid ones "in so far as they represent an economic activity" ([e-Commerce Directive recital 18](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32000L0031)). A free game with no ads and nothing sold may fall outside; the plan to sell weakens that. **Disputed.** A provider established in the EU is covered by that alone. One outside it is covered only with a substantial connection: many EU users relative to population, or targeting through a member state's language, currency, domain, app store or ads; "mere technical accessibility" is not enough ([art. 3(d)–(e)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_3), [recital 8](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#rct_8)). At 45 accounts with no EU price or domain, that looks unlikely (my reading), though English is also Ireland's and Malta's language; the clan form's own placeholder, "Casual, EU evenings" (`src/ui/render.js:2416`), is a faint signal at most.
- **Which kind of service.** Storing clan text at a player's request is hosting (art. 3(g)(iii)). It is an online platform if it reaches the public (art. 3(i), (k)), and open sign-up without a human decision still counts as the public ([recital 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#rct_14)), unless it is a "minor and purely ancillary feature", like a newspaper's comments ([recital 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#rct_13)). Clan words in a game may be that. **Unclear.**
- **What a micro or small enterprise owes, if covered.** Micro is under 10 staff and €2 million, small under 50 and €10 million; a self-employed person in an economic activity is an enterprise ([Recommendation 2003/361, annex arts. 1–2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32003H0361)). It owes a public point of contact for authorities, naming its languages ([art. 11](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_11)), and one for users, quick, electronic and not only automated ([art. 12](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_12)); from outside the EU, a legal representative in a member state, with no size exemption ([art. 13](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_13)); terms that state content rules, moderation and complaint handling in plain words ([art. 14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_14)); a notice route open to anyone, with a receipt and a decision that names redress ([art. 16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_16)); reasons to anyone whose content or account it restricts ([art. 17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_17)); and a report to the police of any suspected threat to life or safety ([art. 18](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_18)).
- **Article 17 may reach the flags.** A save is information a player stores. The growth check caps a flagged player's clan attacks at 1% of the boss each (`api/index.js:496`) and leaves their Hydra fights out of the ranking (`:750`), and they are told nothing (P-025). With no terms, a flag also has no contractual ground to cite (art. 17(3)(e)). My reading.
- **What is waived:** yearly transparency reports ([art. 15(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_15)), and arts. 20–28 (complaint systems, dispute bodies, trusted flaggers, the dark-pattern ban, ads, feeds, minors), apart from user numbers on request ([art. 19](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_19), art. 24(3)). The Commission's minors guidelines apply "with the exception of micro and small enterprises" ([Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors)). I found no enforcement against a micro service, but did not search deeply.

## 3. The Digital Fairness Act and the KIDS Act (proposals, not law)

- **The Digital Fairness Act** was proposed today ([COM(2026) 1100](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/0575d59f-554f-4a9e-b54b-e6b717175809_en?filename=Proposal+for+a+Digital+Fairness+Act.pdf); [RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1111/1598862-digital-fairness-act/)): a regulation replacing the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, applying a year after entry into force (art. 40). It binds traders of every size; size counts only in penalties (recital 88). For games, the minors' rules apply where a product is aimed at minors or the trader is reasonably certain minors use it:
  - paid virtual currency shows the money value of what it buys, and is off by default for minors unless a parent approves (art. 14);
  - paid random items show their odds, can be switched off, and are barred for minors (art. 15);
  - pay-to-progress and pay-to-win are barred for minors (art. 16): purchases necessary, or presented as necessary, to progress (art. 3(19)); recital 65 gives an unpaid path made illusory as an example;
  - where a product can be foreseen to encourage compulsive use, everyone can switch off features rewarding engagement at regular times or penalising a missed one, and notifications they didn't trigger (art. 17(1)(b)–(c)). Recital 66: "video games may incentivise engagement by penalising the consumer if they do not engage".
  - A list of dark patterns banned outright sits in Annex I, which I couldn't find. Euronews' report of the leaked draft counts ten: harder cancelling, buried prices, a false "no alternative", false scarcity, pre-ticked extras, shaming a refusal, nagging, double negatives, fake notifications ([Euronews](https://euronews.com/2026/11/09/eu-to-blacklist-ten-manipulative-online-design-traps-leaked-draft-shows); [Agence Europe](https://agenceurope.eu/en/bulletin/article/13978/1/with-digital-fairness-act-european-commission-targets-dark-patterns-and-replaces-unfair-commercial-practices-directive)). A trader acts for their trade or business (art. 3(2)): before anything is sold, the owner may not be one. **Unclear.**
- **The EU KIDS Act** ([COM(2026) 681](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=COM:2026:681:FIN), 17 September 2026) covers online games open to under-18s, wherever their provider sits (art. 2); its memorandum says "small and micro enterprises are not exempted from this Regulation". It would apply six months after entry into force (art. 43); its financial statement puts parental accounts later. It is now before Parliament and Council ([Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/eu-kids-act-helping-children-navigate-safer-online-world-2026-09-17_en)).
  - Minor-safe design is the default, relaxed only for users proven adult by age assurance (art. 8(1)); self-declaration doesn't count (recital 50).
  - Games must not encourage compulsive use, including through notifications the minor didn't trigger and "penalties or loss of benefits for failing to engage regularly" (art. 15(1)(a), applying art. 9(1), (2)(b), (d)).
  - Safe defaults (no tracking, camera, account suggestions or push, art. 11(1)); no unasked contact, no contact details shown, groups only by choice, easy blocking (art. 12); guardian tools, with under-13s only through them (arts. 15(1)(d), 20).
  - Safeguards against the game steering minors to contacts on other services (art. 15(2)), such as warnings on link-outs (recital 36). Its "video game" is an information society service (art. 3(5)(f)), so the remuneration question returns.
- **Held against P-057 and P-115.** P-057 already rules out paid random items and a tradable currency. The DFA would add currency off for minors and pay-to-win barred for them. Power for sale (P-054, P-055) would be barred to minors wherever the owner is reasonably sure minors play, and the game can't tell who they are without an age check (my reading). P-115's day's floor points the right way, but a one-day bank is still a set time window, and the weekend Hydra and festivals are "regular times" by the plain words (my reading). P-071's pushes for a full crate bank are notifications the player didn't trigger: under the KIDS Act they'd stay off for anyone not checked as an adult; under the DFA, opt-in and switchable fits.

## 4. A minimum plan for one person

- **Write (S).** (1) A rules and terms page on the wiki, linked at sign-up and on the Clan tab: who runs the game and how to reach them; what clan words may not hold (illegal content, slurs, threats, sexual content, others' personal details, contact details or invitations to other apps, impersonation, ads); that the owner reads every new clan and may edit, clear or rename it, or close an account; how to report, what follows, how to appeal; that no proactive technology is used; what a flag is and how to contest it (P-025). Plain enough for a 13-year-old (s.21(3); [DSA art. 14(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065#art_14)).
- (2) A risk note of a page or two, kept with the code: each kind of illegal harm and of content harmful to children rated with a reason (no chat, DMs or images, 200 characters, five clans, each new clan read by the owner); the children's access result (children can use it and a fantasy game may draw them, so treat it as likely accessed: my judgement); the 14 measures and how each is met; the owner as named person; the clocks (s.11(6)(f)); a yearly review, and one before any chat, feed or clan edit. (3) P-097's privacy page beside it, with the same address. (4) A log of each report, edit and removal: date, what, why, reply (s.23; art. 17).
- **Switch on (S–M).** (5) P-110's address on the wiki and in Settings, and its Report link on each clan's row and header, pre-filled with the clan's id and asking for a reason and a good-faith line (art. 16(2)); a receipt, then a decision with a way to ask again (art. 16(4)–(5); ICU D1–D2, D7). (6) P-113's edits for clan owners, plus an owner's route to clear or rename any clan, so takedown is swift (ICU C2) without SQL. (7) A line beside "Clan chat on Discord" (`src/ui/render.js:2400`): a separate service with its own rules.
- **Avoid.** In-game chat, DMs, image or avatar uploads, free-text profiles, links in clan text, and clocks that take something from a player who misses a window. Each raises the risk note and, under the proposals, the work.
- **What it costs.** IFTAS puts a small, low-risk service's paperwork at 4–8 hours. My estimates for this game, code included: the risk note 4–8 h; terms and rules 3–4 h; P-110's address and link 2–4 h; P-113 with the owner's route and log 4–8 h; then minutes a week reading new clans, 15–30 minutes a report, an hour or two a year to review. About two to three working days in all.

## Proposals from this file

1. **A risk note and a rules page** (Add, S): the two documents of section 4. Why: they are the core of the UK duties if the game is in scope (s.9, s.23, s.35–37, ICU G1 and G3), and cost about a day. 2. **An owner's route to clear or rename a clan, with a log** (Add, S, beside P-113). Why: swift takedown (ICU C2) and records (s.23, art. 17) without typing SQL into the live database (030).
3. **No links or handles in clan words** (Change, S; the owner's call): refuse web addresses, "@" handles and invite codes in the description and "looking for" line; names can't hold addresses or handles already (`api/index.js:489`). Why: grooming is the first risk Ofcom's gaming guidance names, and steering minors to contacts on other apps is the route the KIDS Act names (art. 15(2), recital 36); narrower than the word filter 030 set aside. Against: a clan can't name its own Discord.
4. **A line on the Discord link** (Change, under S): a separate service with its own rules. Why: the game's only link to open chat; the KIDS Act's link-out safeguards (recital 36).
5. **Chat, DMs and uploads stay out** (Remove from the plans, S). Why: each would move the risk note from low toward grooming and harassment, which Ofcom's gaming guidance ties to chat; chat is already deferred (ROADMAP).

## Questions for a lawyer

1. With 45 accounts and no country data, does Fantasy Idle have a "significant number" of UK users, or the UK as a target market (s.4(5))?
2. Which day is its "first day" under Sch. 3 para 3: clans going live (about 11 October 2026) or the day UK links arose? When were the first assessments due?
3. May one person be both provider and the named person accountable to "the most senior governance body" (ICU A2)? Must the risk note be published, or only kept?
4. For a game that can't check ages, is it safe to conclude that it is likely to be accessed by children, at low risk? What does s.11(6)(f) expect of its reward clocks?
5. Is a free game with no ads and nothing sold an information society service (Directive 2015/1535 art. 1(1)(b)), and its owner an enterprise (Recommendation 2003/361)? Does that change on the first sale, or when a plan to sell is announced?
6. Is clan text a "minor and purely ancillary feature" (DSA art. 3(i), recital 13), making the game a hosting service but not an online platform?
7. Without an EU establishment, does the game offer services in the Union (art. 3(d)–(e)), so that art. 13 asks for a legal representative? Does the KIDS Act (art. 2(2)) use the same test?
8. Is a growth-check flag a restriction under DSA art. 17(1)(c) or (d), needing a statement of reasons?
9. Would the daily crate, clan attacks, the Titan's bank, the weekly laurel and the weekend Hydra count as loss of benefits for missing regular times (KIDS Act art. 15(1)(a); DFA art. 17(1)(c))? What would switching off a reward clock mean?
10. When does a free game's owner become a trader (DFA art. 3(2)), and would a capped, optional power purchase be "presented as necessary" (art. 3(19))?
