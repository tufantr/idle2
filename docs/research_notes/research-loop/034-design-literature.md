## In short

1. **The literature agrees on thirteen principles** (below). Fantasy Idle keeps most of them in its first 150 hours. Its departures are of two kinds: chosen by the
   owner (clicking pays on easy fights, the camp is bought by hand, late resets pay little), and unchosen, which gather deep in the game.
2. **The clearest unchosen one is Cook's value chain:** essence is 95–96% unspent at hour 300, diamonds 87–93%; past stage 1,000 half or more of camp gold, and past
   1,300 half of worn affixes, buy nothing (015, 022). Nothing checks for it: the simulator books gold alone. Proposal 1.
3. **Two clocks pay lasting power and let it wither:** the Titan banks three hours and the week's laurel none. Zagal's appointment test, Spiel's guideline and
   Bogost's critique all name this; the crate (60 hours) and clan attacks (two days) pass. Proposal 2 names the rule that P-059 and P-017 share.
4. **New things:** the sources ask for beats spaced wider as engagement fades, and steady updates. Fantasy Idle spends its layers in days for the keen (P-063, P-016).
5. **Numbers:** "+N stages" is the right unit (Spiel asks variables to mean one thing to the player), but it is wrong for armour deep (P-075, P-091).
6. **Money:** every source that speaks of it condemns selling waiting or impatience. Fantasy Idle sells nothing (P-057).

## What the sources add

- **Pecorella, GDC 2015** ([deck](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2015/presentations/Pecorella_Anthony_Idle_Games_The.pdf), [Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022065/)): quick
  check-ins pay by lifting the growth curve (slide 21); running out of cash is an energy system players accept better than a timer, as the choice to wait feels theirs
  (27); offline income grows linearly against exponential costs, so time away is worth less than time spent (28); many goals spread the joy (35); a reset opens a new
  loop (37) and is a ladder, a rung higher each time, flying through early content (43); every successful idle game on Kongregate updated regularly (44); smooth curves
  bore (45–47); what the genre sells is time: warps, cash infusions, instant prestige, boosts (52–54).
- **Pecorella, GDC Europe 2016** ([deck](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdceurope2016/presentations/Pecorella_Anthony_Quest%20for%20Progress.pdf)): clicking is fun but tiring (slide 9);
  active skills on 5-minute-to-hours cooldowns burst income or clear a hump (18); a Quantic Foundry survey of three idle games found 70% core and 20% hardcore players,
  with completion and power the top motives (21–22); AdVenture Capitalist doubles a business at 25 and 50 owned (24); keep old generators relevant through ownership
  bonuses, as Clicker Heroes' ×4 every 25 (46–49); faster cycles make players reset further along, slower ones need a meta layer (51); Nonstop Knight speeds
  through trivial early content after a reset, and War Tortoise gives power without rapid tapping (56–57).
- **Vault overviews:** Bogost on Cow Clicker ([GDC Online 2010](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1013828/)); Howe ([GDC 2017](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024072/)):
  people misjudge what long engagement demands and fall into content treadmills; Legend of Slime ([GDC 2024](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1034473/)): 20 million
  downloads and $100 million in its first year, from rewarded video and purchases; Johnson ([GDC 2025](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1035099/)): idle games that
  support the player instead of demanding. Pecorella 2016's page ([Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023208/)) is a teaser.
- **Spiel et al. 2019** ([NSF copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10174274), six designers, mostly independent): spread narrative beats over long intervals; limit
  transactions in a time frame; free or one price; light on device energy; let players stop at any point without lasting cost; let a misallocated resource come back
  later; show a change's effect before it is made; keep each variable's meaning coherent; key the story's beats to the math.
- **Cutting et al. 2019** ([open copy](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/135461/), N = 1,972 Neko Atsume players): 89% had talked to others about the game,
  78% in person; a rare cat was the top reason to share one (72%).
- **Fizek 2018, pp. 147–148** ([ToDiGRA](https://todigra.org/index.php/todigra/article/view/1754)): four roots of the appeal: attention treated as scarce, reward for
  absence, the checking habit of social platforms, drudgery automated.
- **Zagal et al. 2013** ([FDG](http://www.fdg2013.org/program/papers/paper06_zagal_etal.pdf)): playing by appointment is a dark pattern when an unclaimed reward withers
  and progress needs it; it is harmless when appointments only add options.
- **Buergi 2024** ([Replay](https://doi.org/10.18778/2391-8551.12.02), an essay): idle games share cozy games' safety and abundance but often monetise impatience;
  AdVenture Capitalist's managers are bought with play, while a $9.99 helper in Virtual Villagers: Origin 2 came free for a week, then had to be bought. It reports a 2019
  thesis ([DiVA](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/record.jsf?pid=diva2%3A1481219), unreached) whose interviewees broke off to tend their games (unverified).
- **Abstracts only:** Frommel and Mandryk 2022 ([Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145/3549489); 014 has it); Ruffino 2021
  ([Games and Culture](https://doi.org/10.1177/1555412019886242)): AdVenture Capitalist players write their own endings to an endless game.
- **Cook, Value chains, 2021** ([Lost Garden](https://lostgarden.com/2021/12/12/value-chains/)): an action with no next link is "as meaningless as a disconnected
  doorbell"; pooled resources stop pulling players to the work that fills them; sinks a little larger than sources; let a full chain overflow into another; idle games
  escape their exponential sources by resets; content recharges as players forget it.
- **Cook, Loops and arcs, 2012** ([Lost Garden](https://lostgarden.com/2012/04/30/loops-and-arcs/)): arcs are consumed once; strung together they make a treadmill.
- **Cozy games, Project Horseshoe 2017** ([Lost Garden, 2018](https://lostgarden.com/2018/01/24/cozy-games/)): mandatory upkeep, nagging notifications, intense
  stimuli and timed sales break comfort; churn comes mostly from demotivating mechanics.
- **Bogost** ([page](https://bogost.com/blog/cow_clicker_1/)) and **Wired** ([2011](https://www.wired.com/2011/12/ff-cowclicker/)): a click every six hours; 5,000 players
  soon after launch, 56,000 at the October 2010 peak, then 10,000; a countdown ended it on 7 September 2011, and some still clicked the empty grass. Bogost's charge:
  such games "destroy the time we spend away from them".
- **Guan, 18 June 2025** ([essay](https://ericguan.substack.com/p/idle-game-design-principles)): clocks of graded length (20 minutes, 5 hours, 2 days) so every
  check-in rhythm has something to optimise; a ×1.2 step is noticed where 100 to 101 is not (a blog's reading of Weber's law, not a study).

## Thirteen principles the sources agree on

Facts and verdicts are in the table that follows.

1. **Waiting costs nothing:** absence leaves no lasting penalty, and the pause comes from the economy, not a timer that withers. Alharthi's temporal flexibility
   (`incremental-math.md` A2.1), Spiel, Zagal, Bogost, Pecorella 2015 (27), Frommel and Mandryk, the cozy report.
2. **Each return is a celebration, told honestly.** Pecorella 2015 (22, 28), the Egg, Inc. cap (`robust-and-fun/C`), Alharthi's offloading.
3. **Active play is a decision or a short burst;** the idle hero is near optimal. Pecorella 2016 (`robust-and-fun/E` §1.4), Pecorella 2016 Europe (9, 18, 57),
   Alharthi's playbour, Fizek's drudgery. No source gives a ratio; the log's ×1.5 is its own inference.
4. **A reset pays clearly and the next climb is quick.** Pecorella 2016 Europe (50–51, 56), Pecorella 2015 (43), Cook.
5. **New things come fast, then further apart, and updates keep coming.** Guan, Spiel, Pecorella 2015 (44), Cook's arcs, Howe.
6. **Steps big enough to feel, in bumps.** Pecorella 2015 (45–47), Pecorella 2016 Europe (16, 22, 24, 49), Guan.
7. **Numbers speak the player's unit and show an effect before it is made.** Spiel, Cook, Alharthi's Kittens tooltips.
8. **Every resource has a next link, and sinks stay taut.** Cook, Pecorella 2016 Europe (46–49).
9. **Automation is earned, then takes the chore.** Alharthi, Fizek, Buergi.
10. **A safe place:** no bad state, and a poor choice costs time, not progress. Spiel, Pecorella's no backsliding (`first-session.md`), the cozy report.
11. **Attention is scarce:** no nagging, light on the device. Fizek, Spiel, the cozy report, Johnson.
12. **Collection and completion drive idle players, and they share rare finds.** Pecorella 2016 Europe (21–22), Pecorella 2015 (35), Cutting et al.
13. **Never sell waiting or impatience.** Pecorella 2015 (52–54, what is sold), Buergi, Spiel, the cozy report, Scapewatch's reviews (022).

## Proposals from this file

1. **A pool check in the report card** (Add, S; new). `tools/simulate.mjs` books gold by sink and nothing else. Book essence, bars and gems the same way, and let
   `tools/audit.mjs` flag any resource spent under a tenth of its income over a 50-hour band, and any camp level or worn affix past its cap. Why: Cook's first
   balancing step is to check every chain reaches somewhere, as pools stop pulling players to the work that fills them; 022 found the essence and diamond pools only
   with a scratch copy, and P-063's pieces and new festivals will add sources. A tool; the game doesn't change.
2. **A day's floor for every clock that pays lasting power** (Change, S; new as a rule, carried out through P-059 and P-017). A line in DESIGN and a test that fails
   when such a clock (`TITAN_BANK`, `DAILY_MAX_BANKED`, `CLAN_ATTACK_BANK`, the laurel, any new one) banks less than a day. Short clocks stay for what pays no lasting
   power (mini-games; the festival's daily token cap, which the replay fills), as Guan's graded clocks allow. Why: Zagal calls a withering reward that progress needs a dark pattern; Spiel asks
   that players can stop at any point without lasting cost; Bogost; Frommel and Mandryk's FOMO (abstract). Today the Titan and the laurel fail it, the crate and
   clan attacks pass. P-059 and P-017 stay the owner's call; this names the rule they share, so a new clock can't slip.
3. **Evidence for listed proposals.** P-062, P-091, P-092: Cook's dead links, taut sinks and overflow; Pecorella's relevant generators (2016 Europe, 46–49).
   P-037: Spiel's energy guideline; Johnson. P-002, P-018, P-022: Spiel's coherent variables and previews; Cook's visible cause and effect. P-063: a reset that opens
   a new loop (Pecorella 2015, 37); Spiel's long-interval beats; Cook's arcs. P-027: Spiel's beats keyed to the math. P-003: Cutting's 89% and 72%. P-041: Pecorella
   against constant clicking (2016; 2016 Europe, 57). P-076: Pecorella's check-ins that lift the curve (2015, 21), against 017's 8–27 stages on days 1–3. P-081,
   P-082: the cozy report's intense stimuli. P-057, P-049: Buergi; Spiel's one price and transaction limit; the cozy report. P-107 over P-064: Cook says content
   recharges as players forget (the six-week loop) and that adding capped content is a treadmill. P-021, thin: Cow Clicker ended by design, and some players went on clicking.

**Not carried over:** Guan's loot boxes for every reward (022 prefers sure steps); a currency per check-in rhythm (gold resets; P-059 covers rhythms); Pecorella's
ads, gacha and timed boosts (P-057); Cow Clicker's mechanics, which were the joke.
