# Loot, Item Rarity, Procedural Equipment Stats and Item Progression (RPGs and idle games)

Research notes for the Fantasy Idle itemization redesign. Scope: how well-known ARPGs (Diablo 2/3/4, Path of Exile) and idle games (Melvor Idle, NGU Idle, Clicker Heroes, Legends of Idleon) plus enhancement-heavy MMOs (MapleStory, Lost Ark) handle rarity tiers, base-item scaling, affixes, drop design, item sinks, inventory tedium, and crafted-vs-dropped gear.

Method/caveats: the network egress proxy blocked every game wiki (melvoridle, poewiki, diablowiki, fandom, maxroll, icy-veins, etc.) and the session's web-search budget was exhausted mid-task, so wiki facts below come from search-engine summaries of those pages (cited by page URL) and, wherever possible, were replaced or cross-checked with primary data pulled from GitHub: a mirror of Melvor Idle's v0.18 game data (`Items.js`, `Monsters.js`, `Combat Areas.js` in espoire/Melvor-Idle-Combat-Simulator), Path of Building's datamined PoE modifier tables, a MapleStory Star Force table transcribed from the MapleStory wiki, a Lost Ark honing table from an open-source helper app, and the NGU Idle community guide's source files. The Melvor mirror is from 2021 (v0.18); one spot-check (Rune Scimitar: 2.4s, 7/45/7 attack, 44 strength) matches the current wiki exactly, but later balance changes are possible. All "Inferences" are my own reasoning and are labelled as such.

## Key Question 1: Rarity systems (Diablo 2/3/4, Path of Exile) and the "Legendary = 8x stats" problem

### Takeaway
In every mature ARPG, rarity primarily changes *how many random affixes an item carries* (and whether it has a fixed "unique" power), not a flat multiplier on base stats; base power comes from the item's base type and its item level / item power, which is tied to the monster/zone that dropped it. Top-rarity bonuses to raw numbers are modest (D4 Greater Affix = 1.5x one affix's max; D3 Ancient ~ +33% on the top of the stat range), so a pure 8x rarity multiplier is far outside genre norms and is exactly what item-level gating exists to prevent.

### Cited Findings
- Path of Exile rarities: Normal (white) has no affixes; Magic (blue) has 0–1 prefix and 0–1 suffix; Rare (yellow) has 1–3 prefixes and 1–3 suffixes (at least 3 total); Unique (gold) has custom fixed modifiers and cannot receive affixes — [PoE Wiki: Rarity](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Rarity)
- An Orb of Alchemy turns a normal item into a rare with 4 to 6 affixes (3–4 on jewels) — [PoE Wiki: Orb of Alchemy](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Orb_of_Alchemy)
- Rare mod-count distribution used by an open-source PoE crafting calculator, attributed to the PoE wiki: 4 mods = 8/12 (66.7%), 5 mods = 3/12 (25%), 6 mods = 1/12 (8.3%); prefix/suffix splits uniform across valid combinations (e.g., 4-mod items: (1P,3S), (2P,2S), (3P,1S) each 8/36) — [Decnalabnu/poe-crafter calculator.js](https://github.com/Decnalabnu/poe-crafter/blob/main/src/utils/calculator.js)
- PoE: which modifiers can roll depends on item level, base type and flags; tags plus weights restrict and set the relative chance of each mod — [PoE Wiki: Modifiers](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Modifiers)
- PoE: magic and rare items can only receive affixes whose level is ≤ the item's item level; item level mainly determines what affixes are available — [PoE forum: item level requirements](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2076158)
- Diablo 2: magic items have 1 prefix, 1 suffix, or both; rares can have up to 3 prefixes + 3 suffixes (max 6), most spawn with 2–4; item level (ilvl) equals the level of the monster that dropped it (a sword from an mlvl 50 monster has ilvl 50) and caps the affix level (alvl) that can roll — [Diablo Wiki: Affix](https://www.diablowiki.net/Affix)
- Diablo 2 quality roll: for each quality test (unique → set → rare → magic), the chance is computed as `(base − (mlvl − qlvl)/divisor) × 128`, and the test succeeds if a random number is < 128 — [Diablo 2 Wiki: Item Generation Tutorial](https://diablo2.diablowiki.net/Item_Generation_Tutorial)
- Diablo 2 Magic Find diminishing returns: effective MF for the unique test is `MF × 250 / (MF + 250)` (factors 250 unique / 500 set / 600 rare), so 500% MF already gives 166% unique-find and 1000% MF is needed for 200% — [Diablo 2 Wiki: Magic find diminishing returns](https://diablo2.diablowiki.net/Magic_find_diminishing_returns)
- Diablo 3 Loot 2.0 (patch 2.0.1, Feb 2014): fewer but better drops; affixes sorted into Primary and Secondary with a limit of 4 Primary affixes per item; higher stat rolls; buffed legendary drop rates — [Diablo Wiki: Loot 2.0](https://www.diablowiki.net/Loot_2.0)
- Diablo 3 Smart Loot: about 85% of drops (and crafted/gambled rolls) roll bonuses suited to the class that finds them; a dropped item is guaranteed to roll the class's main stat — [Diablo Wiki: Smart Loot](https://www.diablowiki.net/Smart_Loot)
- Diablo 3 level-70 legendary main stat rolls +626–750 (e.g., Leoric's Crown) — [Diablo Wiki (Fandom): Leoric's Crown](https://diablo.fandom.com/wiki/Leoric's_Crown); normal legendary gloves roll main stat/vitality 500–750 while Ancient versions roll 751–1000 — [Inven Global D3 level-70 guide](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/7435/diablo-iii-beginners-guide-what-to-do-after-hitting-level-70)
- Diablo 3: Ancients are 10% of legendaries; Primal Ancients are 10% of Ancients (≈1 in 400 legendaries), and Primals are ancients with all affixes at max — [Blizzard D3 forums: Primal Ancient Drop Rate](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d3/t/primal-ancient-drop-rate/60367)
- Diablo 4 (2023 launch-era sources): item power tiers/breakpoints run from Tier 1 (1–149) to Tier 6 (725+); crossing a breakpoint upgrades all stats; item rarity does not necessarily affect item power, and the highest affix rolls are on 725+ Ancestral items — [Game8: Item Power and Breakpoints](https://game8.co/games/Diablo-4/archives/414003); magic items roll up to 2 affixes, rare up to 4, legendary has fixed affixes plus an aspect (exact counts changed by season) — [Maxroll D4 Equipment overview](https://maxroll.gg/d4/resources/equipment)
- Diablo 4 as of mid-2026 (Season 13, Lord of Hatred; unofficial research doc compiled from Maxroll/Icy Veins/Wowhead): rarity ladder Common → Magic (1–2 affixes) → Rare (3) → Legendary (4 + aspect; some sources say 3) → Unique (4 + unique power) → Mythic Unique (always Ancestral, all Greater Affixes); item power cap 850 normal / 900 Ancestral with no in-between breakpoints; Greater Affixes roll at 1.5× the affix max, 1–4 per item; "Item Power at endgame is binary: 850 vs 900" — [defessler/D4Scanner d4-gearing-knowledge.md](https://github.com/defessler/D4Scanner/blob/6418927743523e405092c4b658f982fa0e2791d9/docs/d4-gearing-knowledge.md)
- Colour conventions: colour-coded rarity was popularized by Diablo (1996), whose designer took it from Angband; Diablo had white/blue/gold, Diablo II grey/white/blue/yellow/orange/green (inferior/common/magic/rare/unique/set); World of Warcraft (2004) used grey/white/green/blue/purple/orange (poor/common/uncommon/rare/epic/legendary) and was the first to put purple after blue; white → green → blue → purple → orange is now the de-facto standard — [Wikipedia: Loot (video games)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loot_(video_games)); [Warcraft Wiki: Quality](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Quality); [Tales of the Aggronaut: Origins of Color Coded Loot](https://aggronaut.com/2020/09/03/origins-of-color-coded-loot/)
- Melvor Idle has no rarity system at all: every item is a fixed-stat entry (e.g., Rune Scimitar 2.4s attack speed, 7 stab / 45 slash / 7 block, 44 strength); power differences come purely from material tier and item-specific design — [Melvor Wiki: Weapons](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Weapons) (cross-checked against the game-data mirror [Items.js](https://github.com/espoire/Melvor-Idle-Combat-Simulator/blob/87a8073f18cefc9a7cb0f490b20ddb6c1155180c/scripts/melvor/Items.js))
- NGU Idle likewise has no rarity tiers; the only per-item variance is level 0–100, and a level-100 item has exactly double the stats of level 0 (+1% per level) — [NGU guide source: inventory.md](https://github.com/sayolove/ngu-guide/blob/aa50a4ffe38f6dbf6bc1390edd616bee7f6cf668/src/content/docs/en/mechanics/inventory.md)

### Inferences
- Across D2/D3/D4/PoE, "rarity" means (a) number of random affixes, (b) whether a fixed unique power exists, and (c) at most a ~1.3–1.5× stretch of a stat *range* (D3 Ancient 751–1000 vs 500–750; D4 Greater Affix 1.5× of one affix). No mainstream game gives rarity an 8× (or even 2×) multiplier on base stats; Fantasy Idle's Common 1× → Legendary 8× is therefore an outlier that guarantees "legendary copper beats common runite".
- The genre's fix is item-level/tier dominance: base stats (Melvor's tier tables, D3's ilvl stat ranges, D4's item power, PoE's base type + ilvl-gated affix tiers) set the order of magnitude; rarity adds bounded extras. Concretely for Fantasy Idle: keep `material tier power × slot multiplier` as the base, replace the 1/1.5/2.5/4/8 multiplier with a small quality bonus (e.g., +0/+10/+20/+35/+50% on the base, mirroring D4's GA 1.5× ceiling) and make the real value of rarity the number and tier of secondary affixes (0/1/2/3/4, PoE-style prefix/suffix caps). A tier-N common should beat a tier-(N−1) legendary on base stats if tiers scale ≥1.5× (Melvor's weapon strength scales ~1.4–1.6× per metal tier late game, see KQ2), so cap the rarity base bonus below the per-tier growth factor.
- Secondary safeguard from D2/PoE: gate what can roll by item level — a copper (tier 1) item cannot roll "Legendary" at all if you treat rarity weight as a function of enemy/zone tier (D2 rolls quality with `(mlvl − qlvl)` in the formula), or cap affix tiers by item tier (PoE T10/T11 life only on ilvl 73/81 bases).
- Colour choice: use the WoW palette (white/green/blue/purple/orange) because players already decode it; Fantasy Idle's five tiers map 1:1.

### Gaps
- Exact D4 per-rarity affix counts vary by season (launch, Season 4 "Loot Reborn", Season 11 rework, 3.0 expansion); the 2026 doc itself flags a 3-vs-4 conflict for Legendary. Treat D4 counts as approximate.
- PoE's base chance for a drop to be magic/rare/unique (before Increased Item Rarity) is not published on the pages reachable here.

## Key Question 2: Base item scaling across tiers (Melvor, NGU, Clicker Heroes, Idle Champions, Idleon, D3, PoE) with actual numbers

### Takeaway
Melvor scales fixed per-tier stat tables roughly geometrically (weapon strength bonus ×~11 from Bronze to Dragon over 8 metal tiers, ×~57 to the top Godsword; body armour melee defence 15 → 109 → 178) with hard level gates (Attack 1/1/5/10/20/30/40/60/70/85), while enemy max hits grow ~25× from the first dungeon (52) to the god dungeons (900–1300), which the player answers with stacking percentage damage reduction (cap 95%, practical 81–82%). NGU uses no tiers within an item at all — every item has a 0–100 level that scales stats linearly to 2× — and zone sets, not rarity, provide the tier jumps.

### Inferences
- Melvor's per-tier growth is roughly geometric in the mid/late game: scimitar strength 6→9→14→20→28→44→66 (×1.4–1.6 per step from Mithril on), 2H strength 18→25→39→55→79→126→167→201 (Ancient)→340 (Ragnar), body defence 15→21→32→46→65→82→109→122→155→178. The total span is ~11× (weapons) to ~12× (armour) across the craftable tiers, with drop-only end-game items adding another 1.2–2× — a useful template for Fantasy Idle's material tiers: ~×1.4 per tier, 8–10 tiers, then a drop-only capstone band.
- Melvor pairs additive attack/defence numbers with a *percentage* mitigation layer (DR) that only comes from upgrades and rare/dungeon gear, so late-game survivability scales via a bounded percentage rather than ever-larger flat numbers. Fantasy Idle could mirror this: base stats from tiers, DR/dodge from rarity-tier affixes with hard caps.
- Enemy max hit ×25 (52 → 1300) vs player HP growth of roughly ×10 (HP levels ~100→~1000+) means the remaining gap is closed by ~80% DR — i.e., Melvor deliberately makes the top 15–20% of mitigation the "endgame gear" progression. A Fantasy Idle equivalent: tier gear covers enemy scaling on paper; affixes/uniques cover the last multiplicative gap.
- NGU shows the opposite extreme (no randomness, deterministic +1%/level via merging duplicates): drops become "progress bars" (10 copies to max a Tutorial item), which is why NGU can shower thousands of drops without any of them feeling worthless — every duplicate is XP for the item.

### Gaps
- Idle Champions of the Forgotten Realms: no reachable source; its gear-rarity/"shiny"/iLvl system could not be documented here.
- Clicker Heroes relic level → number of bonuses and magnitude formulas were not on the pages reachable (only qualitative rules above).
- Idleon: number of upgrade slots per item and the exact stats granted per stone tier were not retrievable (wiki blocked).
- Melvor's gold/silver trim upgrade costs (the (G)/(S) variants) are stored in the shop, not in the item data mirror, so they are not quoted.

## Key Question 3: Affix design — pools, tiers, weights, prefix/suffix caps, smart loot, which secondary stats matter in an auto-battler and how stacking is tamed

### Takeaway
PoE's affix model (prefix/suffix caps of 3+3, one mod per "group", per-slot spawn weights, ilvl-gated tiers whose top tiers are both level-locked and lower-weighted) and D3's Loot 2.0 (primary/secondary split, 4-primary cap, ~85% smart loot) are the reference designs; every game that lets multiplicative stats stack applies either a hard cap (dodge 75% in PoE, crit/attack speed 100% in D4, DR 95% in Melvor), a diminishing-returns curve (WoW avoidance `1/(1/c + k/x)`, D4 armor `V/(V·10/9 + C)`), or additive-within-category stacking (PoE "increased" vs "more"; D4 "same wording adds, different wordings multiply").

### Cited Findings
- PoE prefix/suffix caps: rares hold at most 3 prefixes and 3 suffixes; magic at most 1 of each — [PoE Wiki: Rarity](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Rarity)
- PoE mod selection: the mod pool for an item is filtered by item level, base type and flags; tags with weights set the relative chance of each mod being applied — [PoE Wiki: Modifiers](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Modifiers)
- PoE per-slot weights and groups (datamined): every mod has a `group` (only one mod per group can exist on an item, e.g., all Life tiers share group `IncreasedLife`), a required `level`, and `weightKey/weightVal` pairs such as `{weapon: 0, default: 1000}` for Life (never on weapons) or `{weapon: 1000, default: 0}` for local Attack Speed (weapons only); global Attack Speed exists only on gloves/rings/quivers at weight 500 and its 4th tier (14–16%) needs ilvl 76 — [PathOfBuilding ModItem.lua](https://github.com/Openarl/PathOfBuilding/blob/81eeead3f6c9ba62f8a02fc305b742d5ecae3ead/Data/2_6/ModItem.lua)

- NGU exposes Drop Chance, Gold Drops, XP-type stats as "Special" boostable stats on gear and has a dedicated "Gold Drops" set for the Time Machine's gold — [NGU Idle Wiki: Boost](https://ngu-idle.fandom.com/wiki/Boost); [NGU guide: chapter-1.md](https://github.com/sayolove/ngu-guide/blob/aa50a4ffe38f6dbf6bc1390edd616bee7f6cf668/src/content/docs/en/chapters/chapter-1.md)
- Loot-table design guidance: procedurally generated loot needs two tables — one of all droppable items and one of affixes/modifiers — and "a good loot table is about controlled randomization": the player should not know what they will get, only when — [Game Developer: Defining Loot Tables in ARPG Game Design](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/defining-loot-tables-in-arpg-game-design)

### Inferences
- For an auto-battler the offensive multiplicative trio (crit chance × crit damage × attack speed) is where balance breaks: each is a separate multiplier on DPS, so stacking all three on 12 slots compounds. Standard mitigations seen above: (1) per-stat caps (D4 100% crit/attack speed; treat crit chance cap ≈ 50–75%, attack speed cap ≈ +100%), (2) additive stacking within a category across slots (sum all "+crit chance" then apply once; sum all "+% attack speed" then apply once), (3) slot restriction PoE-style (attack speed only on weapon/gloves/rings; crit multiplier only on weapon/amulet), and (4) tier-weighting so the top tiers are both level-gated and lower-weighted (PoE phys% tiers weight 1000 → 25).
- Defensive stats must be rating-based or hard-capped: dodge ≤ 75% (PoE) with diminishing rating conversion (WoW/Last Epoch), damage reduction ≤ ~80–90% (Melvor practical 81%, D4 asymptote 90%). If Fantasy Idle keeps "dodge" as a flat % affix, sum additively across slots and clamp (e.g., 60–75%); better, convert to a rating with `dodge = R/(R + K)` so that stacking has natural diminishing returns.
- Economy stats (gold find, XP bonus, drop rate) are safe to stack additively and are excellent low-tier affix filler because they never trivialize combat; PoE caps their tiers at 26% rarity / 20% quantity per affix and confines them to jewellery/helm/gloves/boots.
- "Lifesteal" is deliberately tiny in PoE (≤1.2% per affix, ilvl 50+, weight 250 at the top) because sustained healing per hit scales with DPS; for an idle game where fights are unattended, lifesteal + DR is the combination most likely to create unkillable builds — cap total leech and give it to only one or two slots.
- Smart loot: with 12 slots and class-agnostic stats, "smart" for Fantasy Idle can mean weighting the slot roll toward the player's weakest/oldest slot (D3 weights toward class-usable stats; the analogous idle-game move is weighting toward slots whose equipped tier lags).

### Gaps
- No primary Blizzard/GGG developer talk on affix weighting was reachable (GDC/Blizzcon transcripts blocked); the D3 "Loot 2.0" numbers are from the wiki summary of the dev blogs.
- Current (3.x) PoE mod weights may differ from the 2.6 datamine used; tier boundaries above should be treated as illustrative of the pattern rather than current values.

## Key Question 4: Drop design — per-kill chance by enemy tier, boss guarantees, pity timers, rarity vs base-type rolls, drop rate as a stat, keeping drops meaningful at thousands of kills/hour

### Takeaway
Melvor uses a two-stage roll per kill (monster `lootChance` %, then a weighted table) whose effective per-kill equipment odds are mostly 1–10% for tier-appropriate gear and 0.05–0.3% (1/330–1/2000) for the best drop of a monster, while dungeon bosses guarantee a chest whose table makes the rarest piece 0.16–0.5% per clear; D3 pushes legendary odds by difficulty (+15% at T1 to +131% at T6, more in rifts) and backstops bad luck with a ~2-hour pity timer; PoE and NGU add deterministic alternatives (divination cards; transform a maxed common drop into the rare drop) so that grinding always converges.

### Inferences
- Melvor's numbers translate directly to an idle-game budget. At ~3 s per kill (~1,200 kills/hour) a 2.2% drop (Dragon Scimitar) arrives in ~2–3 minutes; a 0.28%-per-clear chest item (Ancient Platebody, 8-monster dungeon) takes ~360 clears; the "best of the zone" drops are tuned to 1/300–1/2000 kills, i.e., 15 min–2 h of AFK, and full sets to ~100–200 boss clears. Fantasy Idle's per-kill equipment chance should therefore be single-digit percent for tier-appropriate gear (so a session yields several candidates) and 0.1–0.5% for zone-capstone items, not "drops nothing".
- Two-stage rolling (loot chance, then base type, then rarity) is what every game here does, and it lets rarity weights be tied to enemy tier (D2's `mlvl − qlvl` term, D4's Torment gates). Recommended for Fantasy Idle: `P(drop)` by enemy class (trash 5–10%, elite 25–50%, boss 100%), base type from the enemy's tier (±1 tier), rarity weights that shift toward Rare/Epic with enemy tier and difficulty, never Legendary below the tier's cap.
- Pity: D3's approach (time-based, silently ramping, resets on success) is cheap to implement and invisible; PoE/NGU show the alternative of a visible *conversion* pity (N common drops or fragments → the rare item). For an idle game, the visible version (e.g., "10 Dragon Fragments → Dragon Sword", Melvor's Dragon Claw Fragment 12%/Griffin kill pattern) doubles as a material sink and telegraphs progress on the screen, so it is the better fit.
- "Drop rate" as an equipment stat is standard (PoE rarity/quantity, D2 MF with diminishing returns, Melvor double-loot, NGU Drop Chance); keep it additive and capped (PoE's best single affix is 26%) so it never becomes the only stat worth wearing.

- D3's exact base legendary drop probability per kill was never published; only relative multipliers and the pity ceiling are documented.

## Key Question 5: Item sinks and progression — salvage, upgrade/enhance with failure, reforging, set bonuses, sockets; what fits a solo-dev browser idle game

### Takeaway
D3's salvage-into-four-materials economy (with Forgotten Souls only from world-dropped legendaries) and Kanai's Cube (reforge for 50 souls + 25 bounty materials; upgrade rare→legendary for 25 Death's Breath + 50/50/50) make every drop worth picking up; MapleStory's Star Force (95% → 30% → 1% success, destruction from 15★, safeguard at +200% cost, chance-time after two fails) and Lost Ark honing (80% → 0.5% with Artisan's Energy pity at 0.465× rate per failure) are the canonical high-variance enhancement systems, and Blizzard's 2025–26 Diablo 4 changes (pick-your-affix tempering, un-brickable via Scroll of Restoration, deterministic Horadric Cube crafting) show the industry moving away from destructive gambling. For a solo browser idle game, deterministic sinks (salvage → materials, merge/level like NGU, capped upgrade ranks like D4 masterworking) fit best.

### Inferences
- Expected-cost arithmetic makes destructive enhancement brutal: at a 10% success rate with Lost Ark's pity, each failure adds 4.65% energy, so success is guaranteed by the 22nd failure (worst case ≈ 22 attempts); at 3% it takes up to 72 attempts. MapleStory's 15→16★ has a 2.1% destruction per attempt at 30% success, i.e., roughly 7% of items are destroyed per star gained on average. These numbers exist to drive monetization (safeguards, boosters) and are counter-indicated for a solo browser idle game with no monetization pressure.
- The systems that fit Fantasy Idle best, ranked by implementation cost vs value: (1) salvage → tiered materials (D3 model; 4 material types keyed to rarity, top material only from combat drops so combat matters — directly answers "combat drops nothing"); (2) capped deterministic upgrade ranks with linear +1%/rank (D4 Quality 0–25, NGU +1%/level) paid in those materials; (3) one-affix reroll with escalating cost (D3 Mystic / D4 enchant) — the single most player-friendly "fix this item" tool; (4) fragment/"collect N" conversion pity (NGU transform, Melvor fragments). Skip: destruction on failure, sockets/runewords (high UI cost), and uncapped reforge loops.
- Set bonuses are cheap to implement for a 12-slot layout (count equipped items sharing a set id), and NGU/D4 both use them as the "wear the whole zone's gear" incentive; for procedurally generated gear, tie sets to material tier ("4 × Runite pieces: +5% DR") rather than to fixed named items.

### Gaps
- MapleStory "Chance Time" and safeguard details come from a search summary of the wiki; the exact star at which destruction begins (12 vs 15) differs across GMS versions and the open-source table above is one version (GMS 2026 30★).
- Lost Ark's failure behaviour (no level loss on failure) is implied by the calculator logic (energy accumulates per item across attempts) but is not stated verbatim in a reachable source.

## Key Question 6: Inventory management — stack vs unique, limits, auto-sell and loot-filter patterns, reducing tedium with frequent procedural drops

### Takeaway
Idle games solve drop spam with four tools: hard slot limits plus discard-on-full (NGU), filters that discard at drop time by type or exact item (NGU Basic/Improved Loot Filter), automatic merge/boost of duplicates into progress (NGU AutoMerge slots, Infinity Cube), and auto-collect-to-bank with per-tab sell-all and item locks (Melvor). ARPGs reach the same place through native loot filters (D4 added one in April 2026) and community filter scripts (PoE `Show/Hide` by rarity/base/affix).

### Cited Findings
- NGU: if the inventory is full, dropped items are immediately discarded (they still register in the Item List); inventory items persist through rebirths; the Basic Loot Filter (20 EXP) filters by item type, the Improved Loot Filter (100k AP) filters by exact item with a light/dark toggle per item in the Item List and is "the best QoL improvement in the game" and the suggested first AP purchase; AutoMerge Slots convert inventory slots so anything placed there is auto-merged/auto-boosted (items cannot drop into them); Shift+Click protects an item from being consumed or trashed; A+Click applies all boosts, D+Click merges all duplicates; there are purchasable Inventory Spaces, 10 loadouts, and 16 accessory slots (10 in Normal) — [NGU guide: inventory.md](https://github.com/sayolove/ngu-guide/blob/aa50a4ffe38f6dbf6bc1390edd616bee7f6cf668/src/content/docs/en/mechanics/inventory.md)
- NGU also offers "Filter Boosts into Infinity Cube" so filtered boosts are dumped into the cube instead of trashed, and the two most important AP purchases are the Improved Loot Filter and Yellow Heart — [NGU guide: chapter-1.md](https://github.com/sayolove/ngu-guide/blob/aa50a4ffe38f6dbf6bc1390edd616bee7f6cf668/src/content/docs/en/chapters/chapter-1.md)
- NGUInjector (a community automation mod) exists specifically to auto-merge equipped/inventory items, auto-boost in priority order, and "automatically filter items when they hit level 100" — evidence of remaining tedium even with the built-in tools — [rvazarkar/NGUInjector readme](https://github.com/rvazarkar/NGUInjector/blob/master/readme.MD)
- Melvor: the bank has a "Sell All" per tab (only unlocked items are sold), items can be locked to prevent selling/opening, and bank sorting settings exist; there is no native auto-sell — players use the SEMI Auto Sell mod or userscripts, and a GitHub issue requested "Automatically sell received items" — [Melvor Wiki: Bank](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Bank); [mod.io: SEMI Auto Sell](https://mod.io/g/melvoridle/m/semi-auto-sell); [GitHub issue #714](https://github.com/MelvorIdle/melvoridle.github.io/issues/714)
- Melvor's Amulet of Looting sends all monster drops straight to the bank (it is itself a 4.55% drop from the Spider Chest), and bank capacity is extended by Bank Slot Tokens that appear in god-dungeon chests at ~1–1.5% — [Melvor Wiki: Amulet of Looting](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Amulet_of_Looting); [Items.js](https://github.com/espoire/Melvor-Idle-Combat-Simulator/blob/87a8073f18cefc9a7cb0f490b20ddb6c1155180c/scripts/melvor/Items.js)
- Melvor items carry no instance data at all (every item is a fixed definition with `sellsFor`, stats and requirements), so all 1,010 items stack in the bank — [Items.js](https://github.com/espoire/Melvor-Idle-Combat-Simulator/blob/87a8073f18cefc9a7cb0f490b20ddb6c1155180c/scripts/melvor/Items.js)
- Diablo 4 shipped a native loot filter in April 2026 whose rule conditions include item-power range, rarity, Ancestral flag, codex-upgrade flag, Greater Affix count, required/optional affixes, specific uniques and set bonuses; a 900-IP floor makes anything below it "salvage-by-default" — [D4Scanner gearing doc](https://github.com/defessler/D4Scanner/blob/6418927743523e405092c4b658f982fa0e2791d9/docs/d4-gearing-knowledge.md)
- Path of Exile community loot filters are rule lists such as `Show` / `Hide` with conditions like `Rarity Magic`, `BaseType "Astral Plate"`, `HasExplicitMod "Rapturous" "Prime"`, `Corrupted False` — [GucciPradasPoE/poe-filters](https://github.com/GucciPradasPoE/poe-filters)
- Idleon players describe inventory/storage churn ("repeatedly fill up your inventory, then stash it to your bank") and treat the paid Auto-Loot pack as near-mandatory (anecdotal Steam reviews) — [Steam reviews mirrored in soma-aihara/game_finder](https://github.com/soma-aihara/game_finder/blob/78b4c0851d10a3b722b967cab5e8d76ebc75fddb/public/data/reviews/1476970.json)

### Inferences
- Procedural (non-stacking) drops need a decision made *at drop time*, not in the bag: D4's filter conditions (rarity, item power floor, affix presence, GA count) are the right vocabulary for Fantasy Idle — e.g., "auto-salvage anything below rarity X or below my equipped tier for that slot unless it has affix Y". NGU's light/dark per-item toggle and its "protect" flag are the minimum UI.
- Keep the instance count small: cap the loot bag (NGU-style) at something like 20–40 items with overflow auto-salvaged (not discarded) so nothing is lost, and stack all materials/currency. Melvor's fully-stackable design is only possible because items have no rolls; Fantasy Idle has rolls, so the bag/bank split (rolled items limited, materials unlimited) is the workable hybrid.
- "Upgrade if better, salvage otherwise" as a one-click/auto rule (compare the candidate's base + affix score against the equipped item per slot) reproduces NGU's AutoMerge feel for a rolled-gear game and is what NGUInjector had to bolt on.

### Gaps
- Idleon's storage chest sizes, Auto-Loot mechanics and item filters could not be documented from a primary source (wiki blocked; only player anecdotes).
- Melvor's bank-slot purchase cost curve was not retrievable.

## Key Question 7: Crafting vs drops — how Melvor and ARPGs keep both relevant, and a recommended model

### Takeaway
Melvor's data shows a clean split: the entire metal ladder (Smithing 1–99), bows/crossbows (Fletching 5–95), dragonhide (Crafting 63–84) and staves/wands (Runecrafting 1–95) are craftable, while the Black/Ice/Ancient/Godsword/Slayer/Miolite bands and every best-in-slot piece are drop- or dungeon-chest-exclusive; of 609 equipment items, 236 are craftable, 152 drop from monsters, 144 come from chests, and only 87 are both craftable and dropped. ARPGs make crafting relevant by letting the player choose the base deterministically (PoE base type + currency/essences, D3 cube upgrade-rare of a chosen type, D4 Horadric Cube add/remove affix) while combat supplies the random affixes, uniques and the rare materials. The model for Fantasy Idle is therefore: crafting sets the base (tier, slot, guaranteed baseline), combat supplies rarity/affix "enhancement" materials, fragments and drop-only uniques.

### Inferences
- Recommended Fantasy Idle model (inference, synthesizing the above):
  1. Crafting (smithing) produces a *deterministic base*: material tier × slot, always Common-to-Uncommon quality with no variance penalty — this is Melvor's ladder and PoE's "buy the base you want". Crafting should be the reliable way to reach the tier appropriate to your skill level, exactly as Melvor's smithing levels track combat levels (Rune at Smithing 70–87 for Attack 40; Dragon at 85–99 for Attack 60).
  2. Combat supplies three things crafting cannot: (a) rarity/affix materials (D3's Forgotten Soul rule — the top enhancement material only drops from monsters, so combat is mandatory for Rare+/Epic/Legendary quality), (b) enemy-tier-gated random drops whose base is the enemy's tier and whose rarity weights scale with enemy tier (D2's `mlvl − qlvl` term; Melvor knights dropping their own metal), and (c) drop-only capstone items above the craftable ceiling (Melvor's Ancient/God band, ~1.1–1.5× the top craftable stats, at 0.2–1% per boss clear).
  3. Make the two interlock: crafted bases are the input to enhancement (D4 masterworking/tempering, PoE essences: "your base + monster materials → rolled affixes"), and dropped gear salvages into those same materials (D3), so a drop is never worthless — it is either an upgrade or fuel.
  4. Use fragment/collection pity for the capstone uniques (Melvor claw fragments, NGU transforms) so long AFK grinds converge visibly.
- The Melvor split also answers *which* combat drops should be equipment: monsters drop (i) their own tier's craftable gear at low rates (a slow catch-up path for players who neglect crafting), (ii) bulk consumables/materials at high rates (ammo, hides, bars, seeds) that feed crafting, and (iii) one signature rare per area. A 100%-loot-chance monster whose table is 99.5% material and 0.5% unique (Melvor's Shaman/Necromancer/Elementalist pattern) is the cheapest way to make every kill "drop something" without flooding the bag with gear.

### Gaps
- Melvor's shop-bought gear (e.g., Slayer gear costing slayer coins, skillcapes) is not in the item data and is omitted from the craft/drop split counts.
- No developer statement from Grinding Gear Games or Blizzard on the intended craft-vs-drop balance was reachable; the ARPG points above are documented mechanics, and the balance interpretation is my inference.
