## KQ1. Clans/guilds in idle games: what players do daily, how contribution is measured, how rewards are distributed, and what backend each needs

### Takeaway
Every successful idle-game guild is an **asynchronous** system: a daily login/contribution trickle, a shared meter (guild points, activity points, boss HP) that officers spend or that unlocks group content, and per-member rewards keyed to individual contribution rank. Real-time chat is an add-on, not the core; two of the most respected solo/small-team idle games (Melvor Idle, Idle Champions) deliberately have no guilds at all, citing anti-cheat, server cost and monetization fit.

### Inferences
- **The daily loop is always the same shape**: (1) a login/activity trickle that credits the guild automatically (Idleon 10 GP/day, AFK Arena 100 activity points/day), (2) a small number of boss attempts per day (AFK Arena 2, Idle Heroes 3), (3) a weekly reset with a weekly cap (Idleon 1200 GP/week; RuneScape weekly cap). Everything here is a handful of Postgres rows updated by ordinary HTTP requests; none of it requires WebSockets.
- **Contribution is measured server-side in small integers** (points per day, attempts per day, damage per attempt), which is exactly what a Vercel function + Postgres can validate cheaply: `UPDATE ... WHERE attempts_today < 3`.
- **Rewards are split into three layers** in the mobile games: participation reward (everyone who hit the boss), rank reward (top-N by damage), and kill reward (whole guild when HP hits 0; last-hit bonus in Idle Heroes). Fantasy Idle's "Clan War Raid" mock-up matches this pattern already.
- **Minimum viable clan for an async-only backend** (my synthesis of the above): clan row + membership rows + "looking for clan / looking for members" flags; one weekly clan boss with shared HP; each member gets N attempts/day; the server computes each attack's damage from the *server-stored save blob* (the backend already has it) plus server RNG, so the client never submits a damage number; rewards: participation chest for anyone with >0 damage, top-3 damage bonus, whole-clan bonus on kill, last-hit bonus. Chat is replaced by a Discord widget (KQ9). This delivers the social benefit (a shared meter that moves because of other people) with zero real-time infrastructure.
- **Why Melvor and Idle Champions abstain** is not that guilds do not retain players; it is that a client-simulated game cannot make social rewards fair without either server authority (cost) or accepting cheating. That constraint applies to Fantasy Idle exactly, so any clan feature should keep rewards low-stakes (cosmetic titles, clan-scoped currency, small % bonuses) rather than power that a cheated save can farm.
- The RuneScape citadel criticism ("takes over an hour", "zero GP", "leeching") is the cautionary tale for **making clan contribution a chore**: Idleon's design (GP auto-credited on login, tasks you were doing anyway) is the better model for an idle game.

## KQ2. Shared/raid bosses ("everyone chips away at one HP pool"): submission cadence, caps, ranking rewards, HP tuning, anti-cheat

### Takeaway
The proven pattern is 2–5 short attempts per player per day with only the best or the summed damage counted, individual rewards by damage rank plus a whole-group kill bonus, and boss HP tuned (or scaled) to the number of participants so a kill takes most of the reset period. When damage is computed client-side, every comparable game either moved computation server-side (Firestone) or accepted that rankings are decorative.

### Cited Findings
- AFK Arena Twisted Realm (individual-damage ranking, not shared HP): "may be opened 2 times per day. Within each new Twisted Realm session, players are able to challenge bosses 5 times for free, after which the expenditure of diamonds will be required ... a maximum of 99 times per session"; "Only the highest recorded damage dealt throughout all boss challenges will count towards your final ranking. Players currently on the same floor will compete against one another on the same leaderboard. The top 50 players with the highest damage will form the 'Legend' division"; rewards are "Twisted Sigils, Twisted Essence, and Poe Coins"; "Diamonds can also be earned for everyone in their guild if they reach 'Legend' rank"; rewards arrive by "two (guild and individual rewards) e-mails". — [AFK Global: Twisted Realm guide](https://www.afk.global/the-twisted-realm); [AFK Arena wiki: The Twisted Realm](https://afk-arena.fandom.com/wiki/The_Twisted_Realm); [afk.guide Twisted Realm](https://afk.guide/the-twisted-realm/)
- AFK Arena Team Hunting (shared boss, individual damage rewards): 2 attempts per boss per day (3 at VIP 6); chest tier by damage; 65 guild coins per chest; end-of-day mail of half the gold/coins of the best attempt. — [AFK Arena wiki: Wrizz](https://afk-arena.fandom.com/wiki/Wrizz); [theriagames guild guide](https://theriagames.com/guide/afk-arena-guild-guide/)
- Idle Heroes Guild Raid (true shared HP): 3 tries per member per day; "rewards based on the damage ranking"; "the player who made the final blow receives an additional reward"; whole guild gets coins by mail when HP reaches 0. — [Idle Heroes wiki: Guild Raid](https://idleheroes.fandom.com/wiki/Guild_Raid); [game-maps](https://game-maps.com/Idle-Heroes/Idle-Heroes-Guild-Raids.asp)
- Generic patterns reported across several games' pages (attribution between the four pages is from a search summary and is approximate): "Guild bosses typically feature one shared HP pool where every guild member gets one attack per day, and world bosses use a single shared health bar where every player's damage chips the same health" — [Ascension Tower devlog v1.4 (itch, small dev)](https://andr3mar.itch.io/ascension-tower/devlog/1629968/v14-world-and-guild-bosses); "Major world bosses have HP that grows with the crowd around them ... anyone alive within 10 tiles counts, regardless of party, guild, or contribution" — [MajesticMU: Boss HP Scaling](https://majesticmu.com/guides/boss-hp-scaling); "The lord with the highest damage can multiply the guild's collective rewards by 1 to 10 times depending on their damage contribution" — [Top Heroes wiki: Guild Boss](https://topheroes1.fandom.com/wiki/Guild_Boss); "each member receives rewards based on the boss level and individual player contribution, with player ranking based on damage dealt to the boss" — [Hero Wars wiki: Guild Raid](https://hero-wars.fandom.com/wiki/Guild/Asgard/Guild_Raid)
- Anti-cheat reality for client-simulated games: Firestone Online Idle RPG "was released into early access [as] a single player client-side game with a few social mechanics like chat and leaderboards, with everything in the game able to be cheated using cheat engine. However, the game architecture has been reworked, with 99% of the game now server-side ... The only thing that can be cheated is the progress in the main battle as it wouldn't make sense to have these insane number of calculations on the servers"; "multiplayer things will give rewards server-side, so that part will be secure"; cheaters "permanently banned and out of the leaderboard in less than 24 hours". — [Steam: Firestone discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1013320/discussions/0/4363502774085386591); [Steam: Anti cheat even working?](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1013320/discussions/0/1630790506927742503/)
- On client-side score protection: "Everything needed to compute a hash — the algorithm, field order, and salt — has to be in the game, and the salt can be found in plain text next to the API key in the exported build"; "If your leaderboard is decoration — a jam game, a promo page, a portfolio piece — client-side is fine ... If something of value depends on the ranking, no amount of client-side hashing will save you." — [Marvin Advergames devlog: Your leaderboard's anti-cheat probably does nothing](https://marvinadvergames.itch.io/cloud-leaderboard-kit-for-construct-3/devlog/1642569/your-leaderboards-anti-cheat-probably-does-nothing-heres-what-actually-works)
- "To secure your game, you need to flip the trust model and go server-authoritative. With server-side validation, stat and leaderboard spoofing is prevented because the server records the authoritative score; the client cannot submit one." — [AccelByte: Server-authoritative logic](https://accelbyte.io/blog/server-authoritative-logic-to-prevent-cheating)

### Inferences
- **Submission cadence**: 2–5 attempts per day per member is the industry norm; each attempt is one HTTP POST. For a clan of 30 that is at most ~150 writes/day per clan — trivially cheap on Postgres.
- **Per-member caps** double as anti-cheat and as fairness: a whale or a cheater can only contribute N attempts; ranking by "best single attempt" (Twisted Realm) is more cheat-resistant than summing because one implausible outlier is easy to flag, while summed damage rewards grinding.
- **Boss HP tuning** (inference from the patterns above; no game published its formula): set `boss_hp = expected_attack_damage × attempts_per_day × active_members × days_in_cycle × k` with k≈0.6–0.8 so an average clan kills the boss in ~60–80% of the cycle; either scale HP by member count at spawn time (MajesticMU-style "grows with the crowd") or offer tiered bosses per clan size. Because Fantasy Idle's backend already stores save blobs, "expected_attack_damage" can be derived server-side from each member's stored combat stats at spawn time.
- **Anti-cheat for Fantasy Idle specifically**: do not accept a client-submitted damage number. Have the endpoint compute damage from the server-stored save (stats, gear, max stage) with server RNG. That moves the cheat surface to "editing the save blob", which the server can bound with plausibility rules (max stage vs. total play time, XP/hour ceilings the sim itself cannot exceed) and with low-stakes rewards. This is the same split Firestone landed on: main battle client-side, multiplayer rewards server-side.
- **Last-hit bonus** and "top-3" rewards create login spikes near boss death; a weekly cadence with a Sunday-evening reset (in the clan's majority timezone) is the lowest-effort way to give the mock "Clan War Raid" a rhythm.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives an explicit HP-scaling formula relative to member count for any idle game; the k≈0.6–0.8 rule above is my inference, not a cited number.
- Could not retrieve Idle Heroes raid boss HP values or how boss level scales with guild level.
- No source found on deterministic-replay validation for browser idle games (search budget ran out before this query).

## KQ3. Dungeons/raids as authored endgame in idle games (Melvor, Idleon, NGU, Idle Slayer)

### Takeaway
Authored dungeons replace "infinite stage" scaling with finite, repeatable content whose reward is guaranteed on completion and whose *completion count* unlocks further content; this is client-only, needs no backend, and is the cheapest high-value endgame addition available to Fantasy Idle. Party/multiplayer dungeons (Idleon) are the one variant with a poor track record.

### Cited Findings
- Melvor Idle: "For all dungeons, except God Dungeons, there is no reward for killing individual Monsters in the dungeon and instead the reward is triggered on the completion of each dungeon. For God Dungeons, Elemental Shards are rewarded after each killed monster." — [Melvor wiki: Dungeons](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Dungeons)
- Completion-count unlocks: "After 100 completions of the Air God Dungeon, Gust from the Ancient Magicks spellbook will be unlocked"; "After 100 completions of Volcanic Cave, the Slicing Winds, Icicle Volley and Ignite spells ... will be unlocked"; "Completing the Air God Dungeon 500 times unlocks a township task." — [Melvor wiki: Air God Dungeon](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Air_God_Dungeon); [Melvor wiki: Volcanic Cave](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Volcanic_Cave)
- In-dungeon rules: "You cannot swap food or gear inside a dungeon unless you buy the shop upgrade. Buying Dungeon Equipment Swapping in the Shop for 30,000,000 GP removes the swap lock." — [Main Gamers: Melvor dungeon swap](https://www.maingamers.com/games/melvor-idle/posts/melvor-idle-dungeon-swap-god-order)

### Inferences
- **The completion loop** that works: fixed monster sequence → boss → guaranteed chest on completion (no per-kill loot, which prevents "farm the first room"), plus milestone unlocks at N completions (Melvor uses 100 and 500) that turn a dungeon into a long-tail goal without new content. Fantasy Idle can implement this entirely client-side as data: `{monsters:[...], boss, chestTable, milestones:{100:unlockX}}`.
- **Rules that create decision-making cheaply**: no gear/food swap inside (Melvor), stats reset inside (Idleon), a once-only capstone with a special modifier (Into the Mist), a cooldown-gated titan (NGU: 60-minute cooldown) — each is a one-line rule on top of the existing combat loop.
- **NGU's titan cooldown** is the single-player equivalent of the "attempts per day" cap in guild bosses; it converts an idle game's infinite time into scarce, scheduled decisions and is a natural bridge to a later clan boss (same code path: limited attempts, reward on kill).
- **Avoid synchronous party dungeons**: Idleon's is the only multiplayer dungeon among the comparables and its recurring problems are matchmaking friction and party-state bugs, both of which require real-time presence. A "clan dungeon" where members contribute asynchronously to floors (shared progress meter) would keep the group flavour without the lobby.
- Effort for Fantasy Idle (plain JS): first dungeon system with 3–4 dungeons and a chest table ≈ S–M (data schema + one UI screen reusing the combat renderer); a Golbin-Raid-style endless-wave minigame with its own shop ≈ M; a NGU-style cooldown titan ≈ S.

### Gaps
- Melvor's total number of dungeons and full chest tables were not retrieved (wiki blocked); the "no food" rule mentioned in the brief was not confirmed for any specific Melvor dungeon — the sources found describe a food/gear *swap* lock rather than a no-food rule.
- Idleon's "worlds" structure and dungeon rank/level tables were not retrieved.

## KQ4. Events and seasons: mechanics, server-driven vs client date checks, retention evidence vs dev cost

### Takeaway
Idle-game events are almost always "collect a capped daily currency from things you already do, spend it in an event shop for permanent bonuses" running 2–3 weeks; Cookie Clicker shows a client-clock-driven season is fine for a single-player game, while games with shared rewards drive events from the server. Industry data says >90% of top-grossing mobile games run seasonal events, but no source quantified the retention lift for a solo browser game.

### Inferences
- **Requirements**: for a single-player idle game, a season can be a client date check plus a config block (Cookie Clicker). The moment an event feeds a leaderboard or a clan meter, the start/end and the drop caps must come from the server (server time, per-account daily counters), because a client clock is user-controlled.
- **Cheapest server-driven design for Vercel**: one `GET /api/events` returning a JSON list `{id, startsAt, endsAt, modifiers, dropCapPerDay, shop:[...]}` (or a static JSON file with the server's timestamp header) and, for capped drops, one `POST /api/events/:id/claim` that increments a per-account counter — no cron needed because the event window is checked at request time.
- **Reward structure that recurs in every example**: capped daily drop (50/day), a guaranteed milestone (500 boxes → nametag; 59 plays → jackpot), an event shop with *permanent* small bonuses or cosmetics, and carry-over of unspent points to the next event (Idleon) so nothing feels wasted.
- **Dev cost vs benefit**: Melvor's yearly birthday event and Idle Champions' Time Gates show that a solo/small team runs *few* events and reuses them ("replay past events"); a reusable event template (modifiers + currency + shop) written once can be re-skinned each holiday at S cost per event.
- No source quantifies event retention lift for small browser games; the ">90% of top grossing" statistic is about revenue in large mobile titles and should not be over-read.

### Gaps
- No quantified retention lift (D7/D30 delta) attributable to events for idle/incremental games was found; GameRefinery's articles are behind blocked pages and their public summaries contain no percentages beyond the 90% adoption figure.
- Idleon event server configuration details (how Lava schedules them) were not retrievable.

## KQ5. Leaderboards: what is ranked, anti-cheat for client-simulated games, privacy/ToS, cheap alternatives

### Takeaway
For a client-simulated game a global "max stage / total level" board will be cheated; the games that kept leaderboards either moved the ranked metric server-side (Firestone) or treated the board as decoration. Cheap, defensible alternatives are clan-internal boards on server-computed metrics, weekly-reset boards, friends-only comparisons, and Melvor's non-competitive "completion %" badge. Leaderboards also create a consent/privacy obligation for display names.

### Cited Findings
- Firestone (idle RPG with chat and leaderboards) originally had "everything in the game able to be cheated using cheat engine"; after the rework "multiplayer things will give rewards server-side"; cheaters "permanently banned and out of the leaderboard in less than 24 hours". — [Steam: Firestone](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1013320/discussions/0/4363502774085386591); [Steam: Cheaters Everywhere](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1013320/discussions/0/1630790987584250092/)
- Client-side hashing is ineffective; "If your leaderboard is decoration ... client-side is fine ... If something of value depends on the ranking, no amount of client-side hashing will save you." — [Marvin Advergames devlog](https://marvinadvergames.itch.io/cloud-leaderboard-kit-for-construct-3/devlog/1642569/your-leaderboards-anti-cheat-probably-does-nothing-heres-what-actually-works)
- Solo devs on itch.io: leaderboards raise "two major problems: hosting fees and security, as it would be very difficult to prevent people from submitting fake scores"; "Any single-player game with a leaderboard—even AAA games—will be taken over by cheaters"; some fall back to local (arcade-style) boards. — [itch.io thread: high scores and times](https://itch.io/t/5075021/high-scores-and-times); [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/8791098)
- Melvor Idle FAQ: no leaderboards planned; the community instead uses Completion Log percentages in Discord usernames. — [Melvor wiki: FAQ](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/FAQ); [Melvor wiki: Completion Log](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Completion_Log)
- Design guidance: "infinite leaderboards can feel insurmountable to newcomers"; "layered leaderboards showing this week's leaderboard, this month's leaderboard, and the all-time leaderboard"; Zynga's friends-only boards made competition "feel fair because it was personal". — [Yu-kai Chou: Leaderboard design](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/leaderboard-design-definitive-guide-octalysis/)
- Seasonal reset design: "soft resets, where ratings compress toward the middle, often reduce that chaos while still giving a sense of a new start"; an idle game (Cookie Empire) "uses a seasonal soft reset to ensure everyone starts equally while keeping lifetime cookies and achievements never reset". — [ICN: Building a fair ranked ladder](https://icn.com/en-jo/blog/building-a-fair-ranked-ladder-in-games); [Cookie Empire on itch.io](https://peralstudio.itch.io/cookie-empire-idle-cookie-clicker)
- Event/weekly leaderboards are a standard backend primitive (Heroic Labs Hiro "Event Leaderboards"). — [Heroic Labs docs](https://heroiclabs.com/docs/hiro/concepts/event-leaderboards/)
- Privacy: Unity's leaderboard docs state apps "must obtain user consent prior to uploading users' scores to a global leaderboard, making it clear that their personal data will be uploaded"; "For users under 16 in the EEA/UK, parental consent is required"; under GDPR the platform is Processor and "developers are Independent Controllers" who must determine the legal basis. — [Unity Leaderboards: Privacy overview](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/leaderboards/privacy-and-consent/overview)
- Apple developer forum thread on Game Center leaderboard privacy (display names visible to others). — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/730490)
- Idle Garden lets players "inspect other players directly from the leaderboard to see what gear they're using" (a social use of boards beyond ranking). — [Idle Garden devlog 7](https://sandsnowgames.itch.io/idlegarden/devlog/1288009/devlog-7-multiplayer-dungeons-distilling-loot-rework-the-great-wipe)

### Inferences
- **What to rank**: only metrics the server computed or can bound. Clan-boss damage (server-computed, KQ2) is rankable; "max stage" or "total level" from a client save is not, unless it is explicitly decorative and unrewarded.
- **Cheap defensible boards** for Fantasy Idle: (a) clan-internal weekly damage board; (b) "clans this week" board ranked by boss kills/damage (all server-side); (c) friends/clan comparison of completion % (Melvor pattern) with no rewards; (d) weekly-reset boards so a cheater's damage is bounded in time and the board self-heals.
- **Anti-cheat budget**: plausibility bounds derived from the game's own max rates (the sim is deterministic in expectation: XP/hour, kills/hour ceilings) + rate limits + a manual "hide from boards" flag are proportionate for a hobby game; heavier measures (full server sim) are what Melvor refused to pay for.
- **Privacy/ToS**: leaderboards require an opt-in display name, a privacy-policy line about public display, and an under-16 consideration; default the account to "hidden" and let players opt in. This is a few hours of work but must exist before the first public board.

### Gaps
- No source specific to browser idle games quantified how quickly public boards get cheated or what share of entries are fraudulent.
- No source retrieved on deterministic replay verification (checking a submitted run by re-simulating on the server); search budget ran out.

## KQ6. Pets/companions/expeditions: cheap long-tail goals

### Takeaway
Melvor's pets are the cheapest long-tail system in the genre: one formula, one small permanent bonus per pet, no backend; expected time-to-pet is `25,000,000 / virtual_level` seconds regardless of action speed (≈70 hours at level 99). Idleon's companions are a gacha with account-wide bonuses (and a pay-for-power reputation); Idle Slayer's minions are timed expeditions that mature into a prestige-like reset.

### Cited Findings

- Idleon companions: "Companion bonuses are always active for all characters once obtained. You can pull for a Companion for free once per day"; paid pulls use Pet Crystals "which can be purchased for real money"; "5 tiers of treats ... Tier 1 with x1 odds and maxes out at Tier 5 at x3 odds. - Idle Slayer minions/expeditions: minions "sent on Missions to bring you Slayer Points", cost and duration fixed, reward per level rising to max level; minions can be reset for Divinity Points. — [Idle Slayer wiki: Minions](https://idleslayer.fandom.com/wiki/Minions); [Idle Slayer wiki: Minions and Divinities](https://idleslayer.fandom.com/wiki/Minions_and_Divinities)

### Inferences
- **Melvor formula consequence (derived)**: chance per second = level/25,000,000, so expected time-to-pet ≈ 25,000,000/level seconds: level 1 ≈ 6,944 h, level 50 ≈ 139 h, level 99 ≈ 70 h. The formula makes pets a per-skill "keep training at high level" goal that is independent of action interval and immune to interval-reduction stacking; it is trivially portable to Fantasy Idle (one roll per completed action, per skill).
- **Cost**: pets = one registry (name, skill, small modifier like +1–3% XP or +2% speed), one roll in the action-complete handler, one collection UI; S effort, no backend. Pair with the existing prestige so pets persist across prestige (long-tail across resets).
- **Expeditions** (Idle Slayer minions / "send workers") are an offline-timer feature: start a mission with a fixed duration, collect on return; they suit an idle game's session pattern (check-in twice a day) and can feed rare materials to crafting/alchemy. S–M effort, client-only; if later tied to clans they become a natural async contribution ("send a worker to the clan citadel").
- **Avoid the gacha model** for a hobby game without monetization: Idleon's companion system is explicitly a real-money pull system and draws pay-to-win discourse.

- NGU Idle MacGuffins and Farm RPG's companion/expedition mechanics were not researched (search budget exhausted).
- Melvor's dungeon-pet and combat-area pet acquisition rules (fixed chance per completion) were not retrieved in numeric form.

### Inferences (one paragraph per candidate skill, with Fantasy Idle hooks and effort)
- **Fishing (S)** — Consumes nothing (optionally bait from Hunting); produces raw fish for Cooking plus "junk" (rope, old boots) and rare "special items" (gems) that Crafting can use, as in Melvor. Hooks: Cooking (new recipes → combat food), Crafting (gems, bowstring from rope), later Fletching. Effort S: it is structurally a copy of Mining/Woodcutting with a random interval between min/max and a three-way drop roll; reuse the existing gathering code path and data schema.
- **Farming (S–M)** — Consumes seeds (drops from Hunting/combat/Thieving) and time; produces herbs (Alchemy inputs), crops (Cooking), logs (if trees). Runs on wall-clock timers in the background, so it is the first skill that trains *in parallel* with the active skill, which is the strongest "one more thing to check" hook an idle game can add. Hooks: Alchemy, Cooking, Woodcutting-alternative, Hunting (seed source). Effort S–M: plot timers + offline-progress reconciliation + seed drop tables; no backend.
- **Agility (M)** — Consumes GP and crafted items (bars from Smithing, leather/rope from Crafting/Fishing) to build course obstacles; produces permanent global modifiers (XP %, interval %, combat stats) chosen by the player. Hooks: every skill (sink for outputs; modifier layer). Effort M: modifier system must exist (it partly does if prestige bonuses are modifiers); ~10 slots × 3–4 obstacle choices of data.
- **Astrology (S)** — Consumes nothing; produces stardust to level per-skill modifiers. Hooks: all skills, weakly. Effort S but low interlock (no inputs), so best used only if a modifier system already exists; otherwise redundant with Agility.
- **Firemaking (S)** — Consumes logs from Woodcutting (a sink the game currently lacks if crafting/smithing do not eat all logs); produces XP and a temporary "bonfire" XP buff plus ash/charcoal that Smithing (charcoal fuel) or Alchemy (ash) could consume. Hooks: Woodcutting, Smithing/Alchemy. Effort S. Interlock modest; mostly a log sink.
- **Fletching (M)** — Consumes logs + bowstring (rope from Fishing junk, or Crafting) + arrow tips (Smithing) → bows and ammo. Hooks: Woodcutting, Fishing, Smithing, Crafting, and Combat *only if a Ranged style exists*. Effort M alone, L with Ranged; ammo consumption in combat is the payoff (a constant sink), matching Melvor.
- **Runecrafting (M)** — Consumes rune essence (Mining) → runes consumed by Magic combat, plus magic gear. Hooks: Mining, Combat (Magic). Same dependency on a Magic style; effort M alone, L with Magic.
- **Ranged/Magic combat styles (L)** — Adds a combat triangle and two consumable streams (ammo/runes) that finally give Fletching/Runecrafting a purpose and give enemies "weak to X" variety in the endless stages. Hooks: Smithing (melee) vs Fletching (ranged) vs Runecrafting (magic) become parallel gear tracks. Effort L: combat formulas, three gear slots sets, enemy affinities, UI.
- **Thieving (S–M)** — Consumes nothing; produces GP, common materials and rare unique items; failure stuns for a few seconds (the Melvor formula above). Hooks: seed source for Farming, GP source for Agility, unique skilling gear. Effort S–M; low resource interlock but high "variety" value and a clean formula to copy.
- **Slayer-style bounties (S–M)** — Not a skill in Fantasy Idle terms but Township-style *tasks* ("kill 50 of X", "donate 200 ore") pay a special currency for gear; hooks combat to production. Effort S–M; it is also the natural single-player rehearsal for clan tasks.
- **Township/base-building (L)** — Hourly tick economy with many buildings, biomes, decay/repair and a task board; consumes and produces nearly every resource. Highest interlock but by far the largest scope (Melvor added it as a flagship update), and it needs its own balance model. Recommend deferring; a lightweight "clan hall" with a few buildings could later reuse the idea in the social layer.
- **Summoning (L)** — Consumes shards (GP) + other skills' items → tablets consumed while training for bonuses; synergy matrix multiplies content. Very high interlock, very high content burden (dozens of familiars × synergies). Defer.
- **Ranking by interlock per effort**: (1) Farming, (2) Fishing, (3) Agility; then Thieving for variety; Fletching+Runecrafting only together with Ranged/Magic as one L-sized "combat styles" milestone. The Atlas of Discovery reception (59% positive vs 86% for the level-cap expansion) is weak evidence that players value *deepening existing loops* (higher caps, more items) over novel loosely-connected skills.

## KQ9. Backend patterns for a solo dev on Vercel serverless + Postgres: realtime options, chat, rate limits, cost

### Takeaway
Vercel functions can now hold WebSockets (public beta since 22 June 2026) but connections are bounded by function duration (5 minutes on Hobby), so for a hobby game the right pattern is still async HTTP: polling only while the Clan tab is open, lazy weekly resets computed from timestamps, daily crons for anything scheduled, and Discord for chat. Free tiers (Neon, Supabase Realtime, Ably, Pusher) cover a few hundred concurrent players; the binding constraint on Hobby is 1M function invocations/month.

### Cited Findings
- Historical position: on June 1, 2020 a Vercel maintainer stated "Serverless Functions do not support WebSockets"; recommended "Intelligent Polling ... ### Inferences
- **Polling budget on Hobby (derived)**: a Clan tab polling every 30 s = 2,880 invocations/day ≈ 86,400/month per always-open client, so ~11 always-open tabs would consume the entire 1M free invocations. Mitigations: poll only when `document.visibilityState === 'visible'` and the Clan tab is active, use 60 s intervals with ETag/304, and batch boss state + clan feed into one endpoint. With those rules, 100 daily players who each keep the clan tab open ~30 min/day cost ≈ 90k invocations/month.
- **Realtime push is not free either (derived)**: Supabase counts a broadcast per recipient, so a 30-member clan receiving 90 attack updates/day is 2,700 messages/day ≈ 81k/month per clan; ~25 active clans would hit the free 2M cap. Push only makes sense once polling cost dominates, and Ably's 6M/month is the roomiest free tier if it does.
- **Native Vercel WebSockets** are new (June 2026 beta) and capped at the function duration (5 min on Hobby, 800 s Pro), so a client must reconnect every few minutes and the server cannot keep authoritative in-memory state between connections; for a hobby idle game this offers little over polling.
- **Scheduling without cron**: weekly boss/season boundaries should be derived from timestamps in SQL (`floor((now - epoch)/interval)`), and daily attempt counters keyed by `(user_id, date)`; Hobby's once-per-day cron is enough for cleanup jobs and daily reward mail.
- **Chat**: do not build synchronous chat. Use a Discord invite/widget for real-time talk and, if an in-game channel is wanted, an async "clan board" (last 50 posts, 1 post/min rate limit, profanity filter, report button, owner-mute) polled with the boss state. Moderation is the hidden ongoing cost and the reason to keep chat outside the game; the COPPA precedent means chat must be opt-in/default-off for young accounts.
- **Anti-cheat plumbing that the current backend already supports**: because saves are stored server-side, the clan-boss endpoint can compute damage from the stored save (KQ2) and the save-upload endpoint can enforce monotonic progress and rate ceilings between uploads; both are pure Postgres logic in existing functions.
- **Cost envelope**: everything above fits Vercel Hobby + Neon Free until roughly a few hundred daily actives; the first paid step is Vercel Pro (required anyway for any commercial use) rather than a realtime provider.

## KQ10. Synthesis: which additions are high-value for Fantasy Idle and in what order (my recommendation)

### Takeaway
Ship client-only long-tail content first (pets, dungeons, Farming, Fishing), then a single async clan boss with clan-internal boards and Discord chat, then a reusable event template; defer real-time chat, global leaderboards on client metrics, Township/Summoning, and synchronous party dungeons.

### Cited Findings
- Comparable solo/small-team games that thrived without any social layer: Melvor (no multiplayer, Completion Log culture) — [Melvor FAQ](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/FAQ); Idle Champions (explicitly single-player) — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/627690/discussions/0/4679778856506997292/).
- Comparable games whose social/multiplayer additions caused pain: Firestone (rewrote 99% server-side after cheating) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1013320/discussions/0/4363502774085386591); Idleon party dungeons (party bugs, matchmaking friction) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1476970/discussions/0/3772364949840011552/); RuneScape citadels (capping is a chore, leeching) — [RuneScape forum](https://secure.runescape.com/m=forum/forums?135,136,452,65951030).
- Async guild patterns that work at scale: Idleon GP-on-login and weekly caps — [IdleOn wiki: Guilds](https://idleon.wiki/wiki/Guilds); AFK Arena 2 attempts/day damage-tier rewards — [AFK Arena wiki: Wrizz](https://afk-arena.fandom.com/wiki/Wrizz); Idle Heroes 3 tries/day shared HP with rank + last-hit rewards — [Idle Heroes wiki: Guild Raid](https://idleheroes.fandom.com/wiki/Guild_Raid).
- Cheap long-tail: Melvor pet formula — [Melvor wiki: Pets](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Pets); dungeon completion-count unlocks — [Melvor wiki: Air God Dungeon](https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Air_God_Dungeon).

### Inferences
- **Sequence (effort in parentheses, all relative to a plain-JS, no-build codebase)**:
  1. Pets per skill using the Melvor formula, persisting across prestige (S). 
  2. Authored dungeons with guaranteed chests and completion milestones + one cooldown "titan" (S–M). 
  3. Farming (S–M) and Fishing (S), because they interlock with Cooking/Alchemy/Crafting/Combat immediately. 
  4. Agility-style modifier course as the GP/item sink (M). 
  5. Clan MVP: clans + LFG flags + one weekly shared-HP boss with server-computed damage, 3 attempts/day, clan-internal weekly board, Discord widget in place of chat (M). 
  6. Reusable event template (config-driven window, capped daily drop, milestone reward, event shop with permanent minor bonuses) run 3–4 times a year (M once, S per event). 
  7. Only then: opt-in global boards on server-computed metrics with weekly reset (S–M), and Ranged/Magic + Fletching/Runecrafting as one combat-styles milestone (L).
- **Explicitly avoid or defer**: in-game real-time chat (moderation + infra), global boards on client-reported max stage, synchronous party content, gacha pets, Township/Summoning-scale skills, and any purchasable power tied to social features.
- The clan boss should reuse the dungeon/titan code path (limited attempts, reward on kill) so step 5 is mostly backend work rather than new game logic.
