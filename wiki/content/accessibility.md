Everything here is in **Settings**, under Options; the **?** beside Options says the same in short. An idle game asks for no quick reactions: your hero fights and works by himself, and tapping the monster only adds to it.

## Motion

- **Reduce motion** stills the fight's movement, the floating numbers, the cards and the glows. The game also follows your device's own reduce-motion setting.
- **Effects**, under it, turns these off one by one, each once you have met it: floating numbers, coins flying to the purse, confetti and a boss's shake.
- The monster flashes at most three times a second, however fast it is struck.
- **Battery saver** draws less once the game has been left alone for {{REST_AFTER_MS / 1000}} seconds: fewer frames, and the glows and drifting paintings stand still. It is on by default on phones, is kept per device, and ends at your first touch.

## Sound and vibration

- **Sound** switches every sound off, and the slider sets how loud they are.
- **Vibration**, on a phone with a motor, has a switch of its own.
- **Blows heard** plays every hit, or only crits and kills.
- **Chime** is the bell for a mini-game chance. No sound is needed to play: everything heard is also shown.

## Reading

- **Text size** goes from 100% to 150%, kept on each device. Pinching to zoom works too.
- **Big numbers**, once you have a million of something, are written in letters (1.23B), **Mixed** (letters up to No, then 1.23e33) or **Scientific** (1.23e9).
- Rarity is told twice: by colour and by the pips on a piece of gear. A talent that can take a point has a **+** as well as its green frame.
- On a phone a tap on a number or a picture shows its tip, and a long press shows a button's.

## Keys

- **Tab** moves between controls, **Enter** or **Space** presses one, **Esc** closes a dialog or folds the fight. A dialog keeps the focus inside it.
- In the fight **Space** strikes and **M** opens the map. **Keyboard shortcuts** turns those two off on that device, for speech input or a keyboard you share.

## Screen readers

- Every control has a name, and a badge says in words what its mark shows.
- The battle has a heading, **The fight in words**: a short summary of the stage, both healths and the monster. It stays quiet until you go to it.
- What the screen tells in passing (a boss, a fall, a level, a rare find, a card) is read out politely, one line at a time, with the important ones first. It never talks over itself or repeats, so a long fight doesn't drown out everything else.
